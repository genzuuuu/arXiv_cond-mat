Title: A unified closed-form theory for elastic waves in polycrystals of any crystal symmetry
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26674
Authors: Ming Huang, Stanislav I. Rokhlin, Michael J. S. Lowe
Abstract: The coherent elastic wave in random polycrystals is described by the first-order smoothing approximation, whose mass operator remains an integral evaluated only numerically. Here we derive its closed form for equiaxed, untextured polycrystals of any crystal symmetry with an exponential correlation function, giving an explicit dispersion equation at all frequencies. Unlike the integral form, which fails beyond one inverse correlation radius from the real axis, the closed form holds deep in the complex wavenumber plane, where heavily attenuated roots lie. It is compact, reproduces the dispersion equations of Stanke and Kino for cubic grains, extends them to any symmetry and contains the Born and far-field approximations as special cases. It reveals the structure of the roots and their partitions of the coherent field: three roots per polarization, one from the incident wave and one from each of the two scattering channels, without and with mode conversion. It also yields closed-form limits of their velocity, attenuation and partition in the Rayleigh, stochastic and geometric regimes, which show why a single coherent wave has been observed at long wavelengths, whereas more than one can be observed at short wavelengths. These results underpin further theory and quantitative characterization of polycrystalline microstructure.

### Paper [2]
Title: Topologically Obstructed Kondo Hybridization: A Route to Kondo Semimetals
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26686
Authors: Yue Liu, Junze Deng, Haoyu Hu
Abstract: The interplay of strong correlations, topology, and symmetry can reshape the low-energy electronic structure of quantum materials and drive the emergence of novel quantum phases. In heavy-fermion systems, the Kondo effect entangles localized $f$ moments with conduction electrons, giving rise to coherent heavy-electron bands at low temperatures, typically characterized by a Kondo hybridization gap. Here we show, however, that the topology and symmetry of the conduction-electron Fermi surface can obstruct the opening of the Kondo hybridization gap at certain momenta. Treating the Fermi surface of a three-dimensional system as an effective two-dimensional manifold, we demonstrate that nontrivial topological invariants defined on this manifold impose a topological obstruction to opening a full hybridization gap, forcing the $fc$ hybridization to vanish at certain momenta on the Fermi surface. These hybridization zeros lead to crossings between $c$ and $f$ bands, giving rise to nodal points or lines near the Fermi energy within the Kondo phase. Our results establish topological obstructions to Kondo hybridization as a route to Kondo semimetals, revealing a fundamental connection between Fermi-surface topology and the emergence of correlated electronic phases.

Title: Off-diagonal long-range order and $p$-wave pairing in one dimension from critical density fluctuations
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26690
Authors: Matthew D. Thomson, Simon M. Martin, Frank Marsiglio, Joseph Maciejko
Abstract: A central theme in the theory of unconventional superconductivity is that Cooper pairing can be mediated by bosonic collective modes other than lattice vibrations. However, collective fluctuations in reduced dimensions can also have a destabilizing effect on superconductivity, by suppressing long-range order or by destroying Fermi-liquid quasiparticles in the normal state. In this work, we study a one-dimensional (1D) model of spinless fermions with local interactions that exhibits a continuous quantum phase transition from a Luttinger liquid to a phase-separated state. Combining field-theoretic arguments with numerical exact diagonalization and matrix-product-state methods, we find that the quantum critical point exhibits two-particle off-diagonal long-range order and $p$-wave pairing. Our work shows that critical density fluctuations at a phase-separation instability can act as pairing glue for unconventional superconductivity in 1D, overcoming the disordering effect of phase fluctuations and the absence of long-lived fermionic quasiparticles in the normal state.

Title: Topological phases protected by half-chiral symmetry
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26693
Authors: Chen-Shen Lee, Itamar Kimchi
Abstract: Chiral (or sublattice) symmetry describes bipartite systems such as the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chain where same-sublattice A-A and B-B hoppings are forbidden, enabling the protection of topological phases. Here we identify new topological phases protected by half-chiral symmetry wherein chiral symmetry is preserved on only one sublattice, as may occur with sublattice imbalance (e.g. graphene-hBN) or localized orbitals (e.g. zincblende). Unlike conventional symmetry operators described by group theory, half-chiral symmetry is an algebraic constraint on the sublattice-projected Hamiltonian such as $PHP=0$, requiring a new framework for topological protection. We develop such a framework, identifying real-space bulk topological invariants and corresponding topological boundary modes that stick to zero energy. In two dimensions we construct a half-chiral Kekule honeycomb model and show that its unusual higher-order topology protects a single zero-energy mode at a single corner.

Title: When the magnetic force theorem fails: dressed exchange in the induced-moment magnets FePt, FePd, CoPt, and CoPd
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26697
Authors: Vladislav Borisov, Maryna Pankratova, Rohit Pathak, Pamela Costa Carvalho, Erna K. Delczeg-Czirjak, Sagar Sarkar, Anders Bergman, Qichen Xu, Nezhat Pournaghavi, Manuel Pereiro, Hermann D\"urr, Mikhail I. Katsnelson, Olle Eriksson, Anna Delin
Abstract: Effective spin Hamiltonians for itinerant magnets are commonly parameterized by magnetic-force-theorem (MFT) exchange constants, which are subsequently used to compute the behavior of complex and delicate spin textures, for example skyrmions. We show that for systems containing atoms with large spin susceptibility, such as Pd or Pt, this approach misses important parts of the energetics. For FePt, it even predicts the wrong ground state. Benchmarking against fully self-consistent spin-spiral density-functional calculations - in which the electronic structure, including the induced Pd/Pt moments, relaxes for every spin configuration - we quantify the missing energetics for the ordered L1$_0$ alloys FePt, FePd, CoPt, and CoPd. By comparing a family of models that differ only in their treatment of the induced Pt moment, including the configuration-dependent models of Mryasov and Polesya, we identify the origin: the energetics of the induced moments are governed by the exchange-enhanced (dressed) susceptibility, of which the frozen-potential MFT captures only the bare part - about one third of the Pt-mediated interaction in FePt. We derive a general bound showing that no on-site (intra-atomic) refinement can supply the missing strength. The dressing decides the magnetic ground state in FePt, where the interlayer ordering rests entirely on the Pt-mediated interactions, and shifts the Curie temperatures of the other alloys by several hundred kelvin. Computed ordering temperatures, magnon spectra and spin wave stiffness for all four compounds are provided.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26704
Authors: \'Angela Tajuelo-Valbuena, Jorge Hidalgo, Ricardo Guti\'errez, Carlos P\'erez-Espigares
Abstract: Large deviation theory provides a natural framework to characterize rare fluctuations of time-averaged observables in deterministic and stochastic systems. Building on previous results for deterministic chaotic maps, here we extend the Doob transform to random maps, constructing an effective dynamics in which rare events become typical. Title: Geometric properties of Wilsonian flows of periodic potentials in two dimensions
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26753
Authors: Daniel G. Barci, Nathan O. Silvano
Abstract: Periodic interactions can possess multiple competing minima and barriers, whose evolution under coarse-graining offers a geometric perspective complementary to the scaling of Fourier harmonics. We study this evolution in two dimensions by deriving a functional Wilsonian renormalization-group flow for a general smooth periodic potential and its running sine-Gordon frequency within a constant-kinetic projection. Reaction--diffusion and Burgers representations reveal two key properties of the flow: discrete translation symmetries are preserved, and the number of extrema can only remain constant or decrease. Consequently, the nonlinear generation of Fourier harmonics may accompany a progressive simplification of the potential landscape. We illustrate the role of symmetry with two complementary examples. A weak breaking of the $\pi$-translation symmetry allows a reduction from four extrema to two, whereas a non-sinusoidal interaction retaining this symmetry evolves from eight extrema to the four protected by its periodicity. These results highlight how symmetry and coarse-graining jointly reorganize competing minima and barriers in periodic interactions.

Title: Prediction of lattice thermal conductivity of half-Heusler compounds by machine learning and transfer-function calibration
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26788
Authors: Shivam Randive, Ojaswini Randive, Heet Lungaria, Himanshu Pandey
Abstract: High-throughput searches for half-Heusler thermoelectrics increasingly rely on lattice thermal conductivity ($\kappa_L$) from first-principles calculations, or from machine-learning models trained on them. These calculations describe an ideal single crystal, whereas measurements are made on polycrystalline samples, in which grain boundaries and defects scatter heat as well. The resulting gap between calculated and measured $\kappa_L$ has been noted for individual compounds but not, to our knowledge, quantified across a materials class, so the values used for screening carry an unknown, systematic bias. Here we quantify and correct this bias for cubic half-Heusler compounds with 18 valence electrons. Using a curated literature corpus that keeps calculated and measured values apart, we show that calculations overestimate measured $\kappa_L$ in most bonding families, and that the excess shrinks with temperature. Scattering by defects, added to the intrinsic scattering between lattice vibrations, would give such a trend, but the data do not single out one cause. A simple transfer function with this temperature dependence removes the median offset, and refitting its magnitude within a bonding family lowers the median error further, though not consistently compound by compound. Combined with a machine-learning model that predicts the calculated value from crystal structure, and tested on chemistries withheld from all fitting, the method predicts measured $\kappa_L$ in this class about as closely as independent laboratories agree with one another, and provides testable predictions for compounds not yet measured.

Abstract: Spots, stripes, and networks recur in systems with very different microscopic dynamics, suggesting that the competing processes behind Turing-like patterns may be easy to realise. Title: Max-Cut on Token Graphs
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25153
Authors: Jos\'e Angel Alejandro-Soto, Paulo T. Araujo, Ruy Fabila-Monroy, Sergio Gerardo G\'omez-Galicia, Mohamed Hibat-Allah, Md Moshiur Rahman Raj, Mahmud Ashraf Shamim, Ana Laura Trujillo-Negrete
Abstract: Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices, with a positive weight assigned to each edge. A cut of $G$ is a partition of its vertices into two sets $X$ and $Y$; the weight of $(X,Y)$ is the sum of the weight of the edges of $G$ with one endpoint in $X$ and the other endpoint in $Y$. The Max-Cut problem asks for the maximum weight, $m(G)$, of a cut of $G$. The $k$-token graph of $G$, is the graph, $F_k(G)$, whose vertices are the $k$-subsets of vertices of $G$, two of them being adjacent when their symmetric difference is an edge of $G$. The phase-reconstruction problem for Heisenberg antiferromagnets in physics can be rephrased as a Max-Cut problem on token graphs. Motivated by this, we study the Max-Cut problem on token graphs, mainly when all edges are assigned the same weight. Our main focus is to bound $m(F_k(G))$ in terms of $m(G)$. We prove that $m(F_k(G))\geq\binom{n-2}{k-1}m(G)$, and that this bound is tight up to a multiplicative factor of $1+O(k/n)$; in the case when $k=c \cdot n$ for some constant $0 < c < 1$, we provide an example where this bound is off by a multiplicative constant factor. We also show bounds for $m(F_k(G))$ for various classes of graphs $G$. Finally, we consider the algorithmic complexity of finding $m(F_k(G))$, showing it to be NP-complete for a constant $k$, and giving evidence that, when the weights take finitely many values, it is not NP-hard for $k\geq n^{\delta}$ for any constant $\delta>0$.
