Title: Goldstone-Mediated Chiral Superconductivity in Self-Doped Electron Crystals
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12946
Authors: Shuchen Zhang, Zhihuan Dong, Zhaoyu Han, Shubhayu Chatterjee
Abstract: Motivated by the observation of superconductivity in rhombohedral graphene in the proximity of electronic crystalline phases, we study the possibility of pairing mediated by electronic phonons. We focus on the vicinity of the phase boundary between trivial and topological electron crystals, where self-doping generically gives rise to a small Fermi sea of carriers with Dirac dispersion. We compute the Goldstone phonon spectra of the self-doped crystal (SDC) using a self-consistent approach, and subsequently derive the effective electronic-phonon-mediated interaction between itinerant carriers at the Fermi surface. Remarkably, we find that the Coulomb repulsion can be overscreened by the Goldstone phonons, leading to a chiral topological superconductor with an unpaired Majorana edge mode. Our conclusions are robust across the $\lambda$-jellium model and a simplified model of rhombohedral graphene, and we propose characteristic non-linear I-V signals as a test of our prediction. More generally, our results outline an intriguing route for unconventional superconductivity where both electronic correlations and topology play key roles.

Title: Borrowed Planckian dissipation in magic-angle twisted graphene
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12947
Authors: Dino \v{S}krgi\'c, Dejan Stojkovic
Abstract: In strange metals, the ratio of the coefficients producing signature linear-in-$T$ resistivity and the logarithmic specific heat is free of the coupling between the electrons and whatever scatters them. In the materials studied so far that coupling is fixed by chemistry. Magic-angle twisted trilayer graphene provides a dial with a displacement field $D$ that breaks the mirror symmetry separating a light Dirac cone from the flat bands, and the Dirac carriers borrow the Planckian self-energy of the flat band with a weight proportional to $D^2+D_0^2$. Resistance curves taken at eight fields are consistent with the field acting on the scattering rate rather than on the number of carriers. The slope in temperature follows $a(1+D^2/D_0^2)$ without the quartic term that a depleting Dirac pocket adds, and the elastic part varies by less than 5\% between 0.20 and 0.30~V/nm while the inelastic part grows 2.5-fold up to 0.38~V/nm. The thermally averaged Kubo formula favors flat-band weight placed symmetrically about the Fermi level over a single level. Borrowed dissipation keeps the bath exponent and transport prefactor of the flat band's own electrons but carries exactly twice their entropy per unit of borrowed coupling whenever the bath has dynamics of its own, so the field-differential ratio is $2\pi^3/21\zeta(3)\approx2.46$ for a conformal flat band and, in a numerical search, at most 2.73 for monotonic bath spectra without a high-energy rate floor. The discriminating test is the entropy measured in steps of $D$.

Title: Universal Fold Caustics in Anharmonic Matter-wave Lenses
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13032
Authors: Joachim Guyomard, Julia Pahl, Ernst Rasel, Naceur Gaaloul
Abstract: Caustics provide a universal connection between classical ray focusing and structured wave patterns. We theoretically investigate this connection in non-linear matter waves focused by a short-duration anharmonic lens acting on a Bose-Einstein condensate. A semi-classical description shows how the leading cubic aberration transforms the harmonic focal point into a fold caustic, whose local wave field is described by an Airy canonical integral. Three-dimensional Gross-Pitaevskii simulations show that mean-field interactions modify the near-focus density and longitudinal momentum distribution, producing a secondary momentum-space feature absent from the non-interacting evolution. We quantify the information about the effective interaction strength carried by the $1D$ distribution using classical Fisher information and maximum-likelihood estimation. We obtain a relative uncertainty of $2.7\times10^{-3}$ for a sample of $10^5$ detected points along $z$. These results establish a framework for studying interaction-driven departures from single-particle caustic universality and promote matter-wave lenses as a sensitive probe of mean-field interactions.

Abstract: Co-free Fe-Ni-Cr-Mn high-entropy alloys (HEAs) are candidates for nuclear structural applications. We used molecular dynamics with the EAM-21 potential, stiffened at short range, to study five HEAs that have been irradiated experimentally: Fe37Ni34Cr16Mn13 (ES1), Fe35Ni35Cr15Mn15 (P35), Fe46Ni22Cr15Mn17 (Y3), Fe27Ni28Cr18Mn27 (Kumar) and Fe30Ni30Cr10Mn30 (C30). The surviving interstitials are enriched in Mn in the HEAs and in Ni in the Mn-free alloys, and depleted in Cr. The results support direct loop formation in cascades in Fe60Ni40 and Fe70Ni10Cr20 and less of it in ES1.

### Paper [6]
Title: Hidden Spin-Supercurrent Counterflow in Conventional Superconductors
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13076
Authors: Johanne Bratland Tjernshaugen, Jacob Linder
Abstract: Resistive spin currents conventionally vary in space through spin relaxation or torque, which removes angular momentum from the transported spin. We here demonstrate a qualitatively different mechanism in a conventional superconductor exposed to a spin-splitting field. Spin injection across a spin-mixing interface activates a spin-supercurrent contribution, allowing the resistive spin current to vary spatially while the total spin current remains conserved. Even without a net spin-bias across the superconductor, finite and oppositely directed resistive and condensate spin currents arise. This produces a hidden spin-supercurrent counterflow with vanishing total spin and charge currents. The mechanism persists away from exact compensation and remains sizable over broad parameter ranges. The counterflow may be accessible through spin-to-charge conversion, either as a modification of the electrical response of spin-Hall injectors or through a local quasiparticle inverse spin-Hall signal. Our results establish condensate-mediated spin-current compensation as a previously overlooked alternative to spin relaxation.

Title: Planckian Charge Equilibration in Fast Scramblers
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13081
Authors: Jaewon Kim, Eduardo Fradkin
Abstract: The Planckian time, $\tau_{\rm Pl}=\hbar/(k_BT)$, is conjectured to set a lower limit on thermal relaxation times in strongly interacting quantum matter. Rigorous bounds limit both how rapidly quantum information can scramble and how soon hydrodynamics can emerge, yet the relation between the scrambling and equilibration rates remains unclear. We address this question by studying the relaxation of a charge imbalance between two subsystems of the complex Sachdev--Ye--Kitaev model and its Yukawa generalizations, which exhibit maximal scrambling. In the low-temperature conformal regime, we find through a linear response analysis that the slowest decay of this imbalance occurs at rate $\Gamma=2\pi(2\Delta-d)k_BT/\hbar$, where $\Delta$ is the fermion scaling dimension and $d=0,1$ is the spatial dimension. These results establish Planckian charge equilibration in maximally scrambling systems and show that maximal scrambling alone does not fix its dimensionless prefactor.

Authors: Jia-Xin Zhang, Wen O. Wang, Leon Balents, Lucile Savary, D. N. Sheng
Abstract: We study a $\pi$-flux square-lattice $t$-$t'$ Hofstadter-Hubbard model proposed to be realizable in twisted moir\'e bilayers. ### Paper [9]
Title: Quantum Control Geometry as a Metric of Chaos
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13101
Authors: Mahmoud Abdelshafy, Joaquin F. Rodriguez-Nieva
Abstract: Quantum-chaotic systems share the same coarse-grained features characteristic of random matrices, such as Wigner-Dyson level-spacing statistics, yet can exhibit strikingly different eigenstate structure and many-body dynamics, including relaxation, transport, scrambling, and entanglement growth. This raises the fundamental question of whether these finer-grained features probe genuinely distinct aspects of quantum chaos or, instead, reflect different manifestations of a common underlying structure---and whether such structure provides a meaningful basis for regarding one many-body quantum system as "more chaotic" than another. Here, we address this question by introducing a quantum geometric framework that quantifies the susceptibility of many-body eigenstates to physically allowed deformations of the Hamiltonian, as constrained by spatial locality, symmetries, and conservation laws. The resulting geometric susceptibility is quantitative, providing a metric of chaos beyond traditional binary diagnostics; intrinsic, as it depends on the manifold of allowed Hamiltonian deformations rather than on the way the system is probed; and unifying, as it directly connects eigenstate structure with dynamics. For canonical models of quantum chaos, we find that minimal geometric susceptibility, maximal eigenstate randomness, broad spectral response of local operators, and the suppression of nonhydrodynamic slow structure coalesce in the same region of Hamiltonian control space. This suggests that these seemingly distinct signatures of chaos are different manifestations of a common underlying geometric structure. Our framework provides a route toward understanding the limits of relaxation and scrambling under physical constraints; in applications, it offers a means to engineer strongly chaotic regimes in programmable quantum systems with restricted controls.

### Paper [10]
Title: Dynamics of R\'enyi entanglement entropies and Eigenstate Thermalization
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13141
Authors: Riccardo Senese, Pasquale Calabrese
Abstract: How is entanglement dynamics encoded in the eigenstates of the time-evolution operator? We address this question by focusing on R\'enyi entropy dynamics in deterministic chaotic Floquet models in arbitrary dimensions. Inspired by the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH), we employ spectral representations of reduced density matrix moments (purity and its higher-order generalizations) whose decay through spectral dephasing captures nonequilibrium entanglement growth. These representations closely resemble those governing local-observable dynamics, but involve eigenstates in a replicated Hilbert space and observables are replaced by subsystem permutation operators, such as the swap operator for R\'enyi-2. Focusing on R\'enyi-2, we combine exact random matrix theory calculations, established ETH results and numerical tests in 1D to formulate ETH-like ans\"atze for subsystem swap matrix elements in the replicated eigenbasis and their cross-correlations with initial-state overlap statistics. In the thermodynamic limit, purity dynamics is entirely determined by smooth slowly varying functions that emerge from such statistics, paralleling generalized ETH formulations in a single replica. In the limit of large subsystems, these functions exhibit a striking structural simplification: locality of the dynamics together with bulk spectral dephasing reduce their functional dependence to a single fundamental frequency. Our formalism provides a setting where ETH-like statements naturally coexist for both arbitrarily small and large subsystem sizes. We discuss extensions of our results to higher R\'enyi entropies. Beyond clarifying the spectral foundations of entanglement dynamics, these results lay some of the groundwork for future applications of spectral filter methods to the computation of R\'enyi evolution in deterministic systems.

Title: Mimicking the zero-field Josephson diode effect with remanent magnetization and self-field
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13157
Authors: Nathan Satchell, Arjun Sapkota, Pukar Sedai, Kurt Lorenzen, Juan Gomez
Abstract: A Josephson junction whose critical current is larger in one direction than in the other is a diode. With a magnetized barrier, a junction can be a diode at zero applied field, and this is commonly attributed to an intrinsic Josephson diode effect, in which the magnetic barrier breaks time-reversal symmetry. Here we show that the same signatures arise from two ordinary ingredients: the magnetic field of the bias current in the electrodes and the remanent magnetization of the barrier. In cross-type Nb/Pd$_{89}$Ni$_{11}$/Nb junctions, the self-field displaces the interference patterns of the two current polarities in opposite directions. An in-plane remanent magnetization translates the pattern and brings the resulting asymmetry to zero field, where the junction mimics a nonvolatile diode with an asymmetry of 10 to 12% and a polarity set by the magnetization direction. Reversing the current or the field cannot distinguish this mimic from an intrinsic effect, but the wiring can: moving the current lead to the other arm of the electrode across the field reverses the self-field diode, in applied field and at zero field alike, whereas an intrinsic diode would be unchanged. This gives a simple dc test for Josephson diodes in the current-perpendicular-to-plane geometry.

Title: A Perspective on Topological Charge Density Wave Materials
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13360
Authors: Chun-Tung Lien, Nidhish Thiruthukkal Puthenveettil, Alexander A. Balandin, Md Shafayat Hossain
Abstract: Charge density waves are among the earliest known ordered states in quantum materials, yet they have re-emerged at the center of modern discussions of topology, symmetry breaking, and collective dynamics. In several material classes, charge-density-wave order reshapes electronic bands, boundary states, defect structures, and electromagnetic responses in ways that invite a topological interpretation. The experimental and theoretical support for these three meanings differs substantially. This Perspective traces the development of these ideas, clarifies the evidence behind each class, identifies unresolved questions and points of debate, and outlines experimental criteria for establishing topology in charge-density-wave states.
