### Quantum Materials & Topology

This section covers a wide range of topics, from the fundamental nature of superconductivity to the exploration of exotic topological states in new materials.

**Superconductivity & Pairing**

A significant number of papers explore the mechanisms and manifestations of superconductivity. A comprehensive study identifies a universal low-temperature fluctuation in the superfluid density across numerous unconventional superconductors, suggesting a new paradigm of Galilean bosonic superfluidity ([Paper 190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08730)). The search for topological quantum computing platforms is advanced by identifying a realistic model for a Read-Rezayi fractional Chern insulator, which hosts Fibonacci anyons for universal quantum computation ([Paper 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31846)). A separate work explores the "zoology" of chiral superconductors that can emerge in Chern bands, uncovering a diverse landscape of competing pairing instabilities ([Paper 7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31839)). On the experimental front, evidence for a chiral Weyl-Kondo semimetal state is reported in the compound CeGaGe, a rare platform combining crystal chirality, strong correlations, and non-trivial topology ([Paper 16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32012)). Furthermore, a theory links the classic Yu-Shiba-Rusinov problem to the quantum geometry of a superconductor's normal state, showing how a non-trivial geometric texture can enforce specific bound-state structures ([Paper 14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31920)).

**Altermagnetism & Anomalous Hall Effects**

Altermagnetism continues to be a fertile ground for new physics. A symmetry-guided design framework predicts and validates large anomalous Hall conductivities in orthorhombic altermagnetic perovskites, even with nearly zero net magnetization ([Paper 53](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33130)). A new mechanism, the "piezo-Hall effect," is proposed, where strain can break symmetry in a magnet to enable an anomalous Hall signal ([Paper 50](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33026)). The fundamental nature of the anomalous Hall effect is further probed in a Ce-based honeycomb ferromagnet, where quantum oscillations reveal a non-trivial topological character with Weyl points near the Fermi level ([Paper 88](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34477)).

**Topological & Correlated States**

Several papers push the boundaries of our understanding of topological states. A theoretical work demonstrates the existence of chiral edge modes that reside inside the bulk continuum of a trivial system, arising not from the non-Hermitian skin effect but from a parity-time transition ([Paper 75](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34000)). The concept of the quantum layer Hall effect is extended to moiré systems, proposing that a superlattice potential can realize a quantized layer Hall conductance of $e^2/h$ in antiferromagnetic thin films ([Paper 103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34941)). In the realm of quantum spin liquids, a comprehensive study of the Kitaev-Heisenberg model on a new lattice reveals a rich phase diagram containing both a gapped toric code phase and various magnetically ordered states ([Paper 59](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33454)). Another exotic phenomenon, "anyon quasilocalization," is predicted in a quasicrystalline toric code, where anyonic excitations localize on specific contours defined by the aperiodic geometry ([Paper 205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17144)).

### Computational & Data-Driven Methods

The application of machine learning and advanced computational techniques is a dominant theme, accelerating discovery and simulation.

**AI & Machine Learning in Materials Science**

A wealth of new tools and frameworks are presented. The "Quantum Accelerator" (QuAcc) is an open-source workflow library designed to democratize atomistic simulations by separating scientific logic from the computational engine ([Paper 74](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33823)). In the search for new materials, an "ANCHOR" framework uses a GRPO composition policy to guide a crystal structure prediction model, significantly improving the discovery of stable, novel materials ([Paper 57](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33407)). A different high-throughput approach combines representation learning and similarity-based retrieval to create a knowledge-driven framework for XRD phase identification ([Paper 11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31888)). For interatomic potentials, the "RuNNer 2.0" software suite introduces highly optimized fourth-generation neural network potentials that can be trained as efficiently as local models by using plane-wave methods to accelerate long-range electrostatics ([Paper 301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17978)). In a novel application, an agentic system demonstrates the ability to autonomously identify magnetic orders and predict magnetization, while also simulating magnetic textures like skyrmions ([Paper 125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35566)).

**Physics-Informed & Generative Models**

A new method, the "parity-selected Bethe-Salpeter equation," is developed to regularize the exciton-phonon self-energy in polar semiconductors, analytically eliminating a Fröhlich divergence for equal-mass electron-hole pairs ([Paper 42](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32909)). A flow matching framework called FLARE is proposed for stochastic micromagnetic evolution, enabling direct prediction of finite-time magnetization states with significant speedup over traditional solvers ([Paper 166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34070)). On the foundational side, significant progress is made in understanding score-based generative models by showing that the weighting schedule in the loss function governs what and when the model learns from multimodal data ([Paper 178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35322)).

### Experimental Advances & Novel Platforms

New experimental techniques and material platforms are enabling deeper insights into complex phenomena.

**Quantum Sensing & Metrology**

Nanodiamonds hosting nitrogen-vacancy centers are used as local quantum sensors to map stray fields during magnetic domain reversal in a CoFeB strip, providing quantitative insights into spintronic device operation ([Paper 15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31950)). At the interface of quantum materials and superconducting circuits, hole spin "flopping-mode" qubits are shown to have their coherence limited by photonic effects, with Rabi frequencies exceeding 100 MHz and a high single-gate quality factor ([Paper 193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10788)). A new flip-chip sensing scheme allows for the measurement of microwave losses in target materials for quantum technologies without requiring any fabrication on the material itself ([Paper 308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28463)). In another major step, long-lived circular Rydberg atoms are trapped individually at room temperature, with lifetimes exceeding 10 milliseconds, opening new avenues for quantum information processing and sensing ([Paper 278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27471)).

**Moiré & 2D Materials**

The physics of moiré superlattices is explored with high precision. A real-space visualization of reconstruction-driven moiré exciton localization in a WSe2/WS2 superlattice reveals that the appearance of new optical resonances alone is insufficient to establish localization; the extent of structural reconstruction and external strain are the key drivers ([Paper 81](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34104)). In a new 2D material database, a high-throughput algorithm identifies thousands of "theoretically twistable materials," creating a vast resource for future experimental and theoretical studies of moiré systems ([Paper 192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09741)).

**Soft Matter & Active Systems**

In active matter, a novel experimental technique using T. aceti nematodes reveals previously unseen collective states where the organisms synchronize their oscillations to create multiple protrusions in a flexible boundary that oscillate, rotate, and merge ([Paper 43](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32945)). A new theoretical framework extends Stochastic Density Functional Theory (SDFT) to "odd" systems with broken time-reversal symmetry, such as magnetized electrolytes and chiral active matter, allowing for the calculation of Hall conductivity in these systems ([Paper 18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32080), [Paper 72](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33797)). A machine-learning-assisted phase-field study provides a workflow to parameterize and simulate the nucleation, growth, and coarsening of precipitates in magnesium alloys, a critical process for precipitation hardening ([Paper 211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18430)).
