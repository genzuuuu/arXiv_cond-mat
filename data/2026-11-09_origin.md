Title: Transformer-Based Characterization of Criticality in Directed Percolation
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08527
Authors: Bozheng Dou, Wei Li
Abstract: Directed percolation (DP) provides a canonical framework for studying nonequilibrium phase transitions into absorbing states, yet extracting critical behavior directly from high-dimensional spatiotemporal configurations remains challenging. Here, we investigate (1 + 1)- and (2 + 1)-dimensional bond DP using a supervised Transformer encoder. Supervised learning results demonstrate that the Transformer can distinguish between the active and absorbing phases of directed percolation with high accuracy. The critical point and critical exponents estimated from the model's classification probabilities align closely with reference values from existing literature, indicating that the model not only achieves phase classification but also yields outputs containing information related to critical scaling behavior. To probe the model's internal representation, we employ attention rollout and define an attention center that quantifies the temporal centroid of the cumulative attention distribution. The attention patterns and the attention center reorganize systematically near the critical region and exhibit pronounced finite-size dependence. Finally, we examined the model's transferability between configurations generated from fully occupied initial states and those from single-seed initial states. The results indicate the presence of transferable information between the two types of data, though the transfer performance exhibits directional asymmetry. Furthermore, when using an equal amount of target-domain data, fine-tuning yielded only a marginal improvement in accuracy compared to training from scratch.

### Paper [2]
Title: Nonequilibrium Fluctuations and Intrinsic Stability of Nanostructured Solar Cells: Microscopic Derivation and Universal Laws
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08529
Authors: Rivo Herivola Manjakamanana Ravelonjato, Ravo Tokiniaina Ranaivoson, Philipe Manjakasoa Randriantsoa, Raoelina Andriambololona, Wilfrid Chrysante Solofoarisina
Abstract: We study nonequilibrium fluctuations of efficiency in nanostructured solar cells. Our contribution is threefold. First, we derive a microscopic stochastic model of efficiency from a two-state master equation describing absorption, radiative recombination, and extraction. This derivation establishes an explicit link between efficiency fluctuations and entropy production, and justifies the thermodynamic affinity through local detailed balance. Second, we review universal fluctuation laws established in the recent literature and apply them for the first time to the real parameters of nanostructured solar cells. We show that the total entropy production behaves as a submartingale, and that its negative exponential is a martingale. For a biased discrete random walk, the exact infimum law is verified numerically in the near-equilibrium regime, then applied to realistic solar cell configurations. Third, our simulations show that nanostructured solar cells operate in a strongly nonequilibrium regime, where negative entropy fluctuations, and hence efficiency fluctuations, are exponentially rare. Over all simulated trajectories, most configurations show no detectable negative fluctuation, consistent with the theoretical prediction. These results explain the intrinsic statistical stability of the devices and provide a conceptual and quantitative framework for understanding efficiency fluctuations around the mean values. They do not modify the mean efficiencies obtained previously, but clarify their statistical robustness in the strongly nonequilibrium regime.

Authors: Ruodan Liu, Ahana Ghosh, Sean Campbell, Venkata Sai Narayana Bavisetty, Matthew Wheeler, Claus Kadelka
Abstract: Basin entropy measures uncertainty about a network's long-term behavior. For every critical one-input synchronous Boolean network with $m$ relevant nodes, we show that the permutation structure induced by its feedback loops determines basin entropy, up to a universal error. ### Paper [5]
Title: Interacting particles crossing barriers: spatial statistics, choice of route taken and role of interactions
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08617
Authors: Gulzar Ahmad, Andrew J Archer, Sergey Savel'ev, Marco G Mazza
Abstract: We consider the statistics of pairs of particles moving in a potential energy landscape exhibiting a choice of different pathways from a local minimum start point to the global energy minimum destination. We examine the impact of both attractive and repulsive interactions between the particles, treating these via either a soft Gaussian or a Lennard-Jones potential. We investigate the statistics of which particle arrives at the destination first, arrival-time distributions and where in the system the second particle is located when the first arrives. We find that the typical arrival time of the first particle can be significantly different to that of a single particle, sometimes being much longer and in other cases being shorter, depending on the nature of the particle interactions. In the case of soft Gaussian attractive interactions, which effectively bind the pair of particles together to form a single larger particle, the arrival time can be much longer, since bigger particles cross barriers more slowly. In contrast, the arrival time is shorter when there is a repulsive core interaction between the particles, due to excluded-area and particle-ordering effects. We investigate the probability distribution of particles' positions through the Jensen--Shannon divergence which unveils the dependence of the spatial distribution of the particles within the confining potential on the parameters of the particle interaction potentials.

Title: Devitrification: crystal nucleation and growth mechanisms in glasses
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08667
Authors: Max Brown, Peter Wolynes
Abstract: If a liquid is cooled rapidly, it may fall out of equilibrium before it can crystallize. It may, however, crystallize later from the nonequilibrium glassy state. Using the Random First-Order Transition (RFOT) theory of glasses, we discuss a variety of mechanisms of crystallization as a route to devitrification. Ordinary nucleation is sped up by the frozen in energy of the glass relative to the liquid. At sufficiently low temperatures, a mechanism of nanonucleation appears, producing nanometer scale crystal inclusions in the glass. Nanocrystallization depends strongly on the local fluctuations in stability, and the transition from classical nucleation and growth to nanocrystallization occurs dominantly through rare, less stable glassy regions that percolate through space, producing an additional dramatic speedup in the crystal nucleation and growth rates. Similar fluctuations in local stability also explain anomalies of crystal growth in deeply supercooled systems.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08678
Authors: James R. Kermode
Abstract: Machine-learned interatomic potentials are misspecified, so Bayesian posterior variances understate their errors. For linear atomic cluster expansion (ACE) potentials we derive calibrated uncertainties from one misspecification-robust parameter covariance, the delete-one-configuration jackknife. Scaled by one conformal quantile from a held-out part of the training set, it gives per-atom force uncertainties close to their target coverage on held-out silicon data and on cracks left out of the fit; screw dislocations left out are under-covered by the smaller of the two models. Where the jackknife fails is relaxed point defects, whose training cells are all hot snapshots: a bias that every configuration shares is invisible to a jackknife, but it can be measured from the training labels, and as a discrepancy term, devised after the property results were seen, it brings the coverage close to nominal.
