Title: Mpemba effect as an entropy-production race:Finite-amplitude bounds on crossing times in Markovian relaxation
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40704
Authors: Mesfin Taye
Abstract: Two canonical preparations evolving under a common final Markov generator can exhibit the Mpemba effect because they excite different relaxation modes. We bound the first crossing of their relative entropies by treating relaxation as an entropy-production race. The exact balance $-\dot D=\dot e_p$ implies that a meeting occurs when the accumulated \emph{difference} of normalized entropy-production rates, $\int_0^t(r_H-r_C)\,\mathrm ds$ with $r_\alpha=\dot e_p^{\alpha}/D_\alpha$, equals the initial logarithmic deficit $\ln[D_H(0)/D_C(0)]$. Global entropy-decay inequalities yield a lower crossing-time bound when the entropy-decay constant is bounded from below and the hot rate from above; we supply both certificates. A nonzero cubic moment of a slowest eigenfunction is sufficient for the modified logarithmic Sobolev constant to lie strictly below the spectral gap. Combining established relative-entropy comparison inequalities with Markov contraction and the actual mode amplitudes gives finite-amplitude crossing bounds without a small-deviation expansion. For an exactly strong, reversible three-state preparation these give $0.1659\le t_M\le0.1674$ in model time units, independently certified by outward-rounded interval arithmetic. This interval is narrower than $1\%$ and contains $t_M=0.1662209$. The conservative global-rate comparison used here gives only $t_M\ge0.042$. The hotter preparation starts $6.12$ times farther from equilibrium, yet it reaches $D=10^{-5}$ at $t=0.233$, compared with $t=0.844$ for the colder one. Mean energy crosses later than relative entropy. For a fixed driven generator, nonadiabatic entropy production governs the same balance. These results concern finite-state relaxation in a specified distance and do not predict water-freezing times.

Title: SKFitter: Symmetry resolved Slater Koster tight-binding models from first principles for s, p, d and f electrons, spin orbit coupling and magnetism
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40729
Authors: Sundeep Kapila, Bhaskaran Muralidharan, Satadeep Bhattacharjee
Abstract: Constructing accurate and physically interpretable tight binding (TB) Hamiltonians from first principles calculations remains challenging, particularly for materials exhibiting complex orbital hybridization, spin orbit coupling (SOC), and magnetic order. SKFitter is an open-source Python framework for constructing and optimizing symmetry resolved Slater Koster TB models directly from density functional theory (DFT) calculations performed using Quantum ESPRESSO or VASP. The framework supports s to f orbitals, incorporates on site SOC and site resolved magnetic exchange interactions, and accommodates both orthogonal and nonorthogonal basis. A central feature is the symmetry resolved parameterization of orbital manifolds, which allows independent two centre hopping integrals between symmetry allowed orbital groups and across distinct neighbour shells. SKFitter also identifies missing orbital contributions and guide systematic basis set refinement and optimizes parameters through weights. The resulting Hamiltonian can be used to evaluate anomalous (AHC) and spin Hall conductivities (SHC), and topological invariants. We demonstrate the framework using eight representative materials spanning semimetals, semiconductors, transition metals, f-electron compounds, and magnetic systems. Symmetry resolved models achieve band energy errors of 9 to 41 meV within 1 eV of the Fermi level. The fitted Hamiltonians reproduce characteristic features of the SHC response of Pt, the SOC dependence of the AHC of Fe, and the momentum dependent altermagnetic spin splitting of MnTe without any altermagnet specific interaction. For EuMn2Bi2, projection guided orbital refinement reduces the near Fermi level fitting error from 120 to 32 meV. Together, these results establish SKFitter as a flexible and reproducible framework for developing symmetry consistent, physically interpretable TB models of complex materials.

Title: Kibble-Zurek dynamics beyond the thermodynamic limit
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40755
Authors: Max Haas, Guy C. Moore, Isaac M. Craig, Kristin A. Persson, Jiali He, Michael E. Hjelmstad, Dennis Meier, Sin\'ead M. Griffin, Jan Schulthei{\ss}
Abstract: The Kibble-Zurek mechanism (KZM) predicts the universal scaling of topological defect densities generated during continuous phase transitions, a result derived in the thermodynamic limit of homogeneous systems. Yet real materials are intrinsically finite and heterogeneous, raising the question of how nonequilibrium universality survives outside this idealized limit. Here we establish an ensemble-finite-size extension of the KZM that describes critical dynamics in collections of independent finite subsystems with distributed sizes. We test this framework using a polycrystalline model system undergoing a symmetry-breaking transition. We find that the density of topological vortices follows an apparent power law with an exponent approximately half that observed in single crystals. This reduced exponent emerges quantitatively from averaging over the experimentally measured grain-size distribution, without fitting to the measured vortex densities. Our results demonstrate that finite-size effects do not simply suppress KZ scaling but reorganize it into an emergent statistical scaling regime governed by system-size heterogeneity. This establishes microstructure and geometry as control parameters of nonequilibrium critical dynamics and provides a general strategy to engineer scaling behaviour in complex materials.

The structured electromagnetic fluctuations of a dark cavity environment can renormalize fundamental thermodynamical material properties and energy scales, holding promise to create a rich ecosystem of cavity-induced ground states and functionalities absent in the bare material. However, direct experimental demonstrations that cavity coupling can stabilize or enhance genuinely new phases of matter have remained scarce. This work establishes a new paradigm for materials engineering with direct applications for quantum transduction, quantum sensing, and nonlinear integrated photonics.

Title: Singlet-Triplet Transition of Rotating Moir\'e Atoms in a Magnetic Field
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40786
Authors: Conor Smith, Yubo Yang, Shiwei Zhang, Miguel A. Morales
Abstract: Moir\'e superlattices can bind several electrons to each potential minimum, forming few-electron "moir\'e atoms." Using neural quantum states that obey magnetic periodic boundary conditions, include full Landau-level mixing, and treat both spin sectors, we map the ground state of two-electron moir\'e atoms in a superlattice as a function of moir\'e depth and magnetic field strength. At strong field each atom is a spin-triplet maximum-density droplet with canonical angular momentum $\langle L_z\rangle=-1$ and a persistent circulating current: the rotating counterpart of the "Wigner molecular crystal" recently imaged in moir\'e superlattices. Deepening the wells drives a singlet-triplet transition to non-rotating spin-singlet atoms whose only current is a weak diamagnetic counter-circulation. The transition moves to deeper wells as the field grows, so depth and field together open a window for the formation of rotors. This is the collective analog of the singlet-triplet transition of two-electron quantum dots. At the transition, the orbital magnetic moments of the atoms reverse sign as their spin polarization switches, a joint signature accessible to magnetometry. If the atoms are instead polarized by other means, such as magnetic proximity coupling, the rotors lock at weak field into a spontaneously ordered crystal of dimers in a pinwheel pattern.

Title: Theory of Convective Diffusion
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40862
Authors: \v{Z}iga Krajnik, Takato Yoshimura, Alvise Bastianello, Enej Ilievski
Abstract: We obtain a hydrodynamic theory of diffusive-scale dynamics in one-dimensional systems with interacting sound modes in the absence of self-interactions. Chaotic systems generically feature three distinct Gaussian relaxation processes: besides normal diffusion, driven by microscopic noise stemming from fast-decaying non-hydrodynamic modes, collisions between sound modes lead to two additional channels: convective diffusion manifesting as an effective multiplicative temporal noise, and an effective additive noise generated by the surrounding hydrodynamic modes. Our theory also includes, as a subclass, integrable systems described by generalized hydrodynamics, representing a pure realization of convective diffusion.
