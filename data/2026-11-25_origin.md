Title: Exact nonstationary heat-excess statistics in the weakly asymmetric Kipnis--Marchioro--Presutti model
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31303
Authors: Baruch Meerson
Abstract: Exact nonstationary large-deviation statistics remain scarce for interacting many-body systems. Here we employ the macroscopic fluctuation theory (MFT) to determine the complete long-time statistics of heat excess produced by an initially localized heat pulse in the weakly asymmetric Kipnis--Marchioro--Presutti model. We find a remarkable nonlocal canonical transformation which maps the weakly asymmetric MFT problem, including its initial and terminal conditions, onto the symmetric problem, previously solved exactly. The resulting rate function is an explicit nonlinear deformation of its symmetric counterpart. We also extend the solution to heat measured beyond an arbitrary observation point.

Title: Condensates and Edge Effects in the Generalised Takayasu Model
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31325
Authors: Angad Singh
Abstract: Recently, in the 1D Takayasu Mass Model (MM) of diffusion-aggregation with input, large condensates and a novel power law were observed in simulations, and later characterised exactly. We extend these studies to the Takayasu model with generalised input, and provide a relation to translate the distribution function of the MM to any model with a space and time independent injection process. In the 1D Charge Model (CM) of a system of size $L$, where $q=+1$ and $-1$ charge monomers are injected with equal probability at each site in each time step, there is a power law distribution of cluster charge $P(q)\sim |q|^{-5/3}$. This distribution cuts off at $|q|\sim L^{3/2}$ in the steady state for long times $t\gg L^2$ and a large charge condensate forms with a net charge between $-\sqrt{Lt}$ and $\sqrt{Lt}$. A distance dependent distribution function is observed around the charge condensate and near open boundaries, both of which act like an edge in the system, analogous to the observations in the MM. The charge distribution measured at a distance $x$ from the edge transitions from the previously known CM bulk power law $P(q)\sim |q|^{-5/3}$ for small clusters ($|q|< x^{3/2}$), to a novel power law $P(q)\sim |q|^{-7/3}$ for larger clusters ($|q|>x^{3/2}$). In the 1D Pair Injection Model (PIM), where $+1$ and $-1$ charge monomers are injected simultaneously at adjacent sites, the power law distribution $P(q)\sim |q|^{-2}$ cuts off at $|q|\sim L$ in the steady state for long times, and no large growing condensate is formed. We still observe a distance dependent distribution measured from the largest clusters, or an open boundary, with a bulk power law $P(q)\sim |q|^{-2}$ for small clusters ($|q|< x$), and an edge power law $P(q)\sim |q|^{-3}$ for larger clusters ($|q >x$).

Title: What time is it in Assembly Space? Cronin and Walker's "The Physics of Causation" fails elementary dimensional analysis (Comment on arXiv:2601.00515)
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31339
Authors: Philip Moriarty
Abstract: Cronin and Walker claim that causation is quantified by an ``assembly index'', a count of the steps needed to build an object. Remarkably, they equate ``continuous time (as measured by clocks)'' to a multiple of the index, which is a dimensionless number. Their argument treats one parameter as simultaneously a rate, a time interval, and a pure number, and provides no timescale to convert index to clock time. This dimensional incoherence betrays, at best, an ill-conceived and ill-formed theory, and fatally undermines two central claims. First, the exponential weighting at the heart of what they call the assembly equation can be obtained only by supplying a timescale, which they never do, or by recasting clock time as a dimensionless count of assembly steps. In the latter case, their ``derivation'' trivially reduces to Bernoulli's seventeenth-century compound-interest limit. Second, lacking rates or energy scales, their account cannot predict how quickly, or under what conditions, anything forms, and yet formation ``in finite time'' is their criterion for distinguishing spontaneous from ``selected'' objects. Assembly theory is thus incapable of addressing causation.

Authors: Farzan Vafa, Puskar Mondal, L. Mahadevan
Abstract: Surface morphogenesis occurs across biological scales, from subcellular vesicles and cells to visceral organs, tissue organoids, and whole organisms. Since morphogenesis often involves changes in topology, a natural question is that of appropriate mathematical descriptions that provide an approach to topological transitions of thin surfaces. Here, we suggest a general intrinsic framework coupling morphogen organization to surface geometry. We propose a minimal geometric model in which smoothing competes with morphogen-induced deformation to regulate shape, while loss of this balance can bring a surface toward a topological transition via neck constriction. Title: Tunneling signatures of the Lifshitz transition in a superconducting vortex
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31371
Authors: Alex Levchenko
Abstract: The Caroli-de Gennes-Matricon states bound to a vortex line in a clean type-II superconductor form one-dimensional minibands that disperse along the vortex. The lowest miniband is separated from the Fermi level by a minigap $E_0\sim\Delta^2/E_F$. A magnetic field shifts the minibands of the two spin projections in opposite directions. At the threshold field $B^*$ the Zeeman energy equals $E_0$. Estimates for FeSe place $B^*$ at a few tesla. The lowest miniband of one spin projection then crosses the Fermi level, and a spin-polarized one-dimensional Fermi sea forms inside the vortex core. We develop the theory of tunneling spectra across this Lifshitz transition. We solve the Bogoliubov-de Gennes equations numerically in the quantum limit for all momenta along the vortex and compute the local density of states with thermal and instrumental broadening. The zero-bias conductance at the vortex center is absent below $B^*$. It has a maximum near $B^*$ with the inverse-square-root field dependence of a one-dimensional van Hove singularity and decreases at higher fields. The next miniband produces a second onset at a finite distance from the center. The zero-bias signal is carried by one spin projection at the center and by the opposite one in the surrounding ring. We then project the screened electron interaction onto the filled miniband. The resulting Luttinger parameter is close to unity, so the core hosts a weakly interacting one-dimensional Fermi gas. Its quasiparticles relax through three-particle collisions in analogy with thermalization in quantum wires and at quantum Hall edges. The corresponding relaxation rates are established analytically.

Title: Three-phase contact lines as distinct molecular adsorption environments
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31404
Authors: Fabio Staniscia, Matej Kandu\v{c}
Abstract: In wetting and multiphase systems, three-phase contact lines form where liquid, solid, and gas phases meet, creating effectively one-dimensional boundaries with distinct molecular environments. Despite their ubiquity in droplets, liquid menisci, capillary bridges, porous materials, and confined multiphase systems, molecular adsorption at these lower-dimensional boundaries has remained largely unexplored. Here, we use molecular dynamics simulations to show that the contact line on a hydrophobic substrate is not merely the geometric intersection of two interfaces but a distinct thermodynamic adsorption environment with a substantially stronger affinity for amphiphilic molecules than either neighboring interface. The investigated amphiphiles of different molecular structures exhibit a common scaling relation between line and surface adsorption in the dilute regime, while their behavior becomes molecule-specific at higher concentrations. Furthermore, contact-line affinity increases exponentially with alkyl-chain length, leading to crowding at progressively lower concentrations and making contact lines extraordinarily sensitive to trace amphiphilic impurities. Because line, surface, and bulk populations scale differently with system size, contact-line adsorption becomes increasingly important at small length scales, where it can substantially alter molecular partitioning. Contact-line enrichment is predicted to reduce the line tension by an amount comparable to its intrinsic magnitude, with potentially substantial consequences for heterogeneous nucleation. Our results establish molecular adsorption as a fundamental property of three-phase contact lines with implications for wetting and multiphase systems across length scales.

Abstract: The spin-1/2 XXZ antiferromagnet on the triangular lattice in a magnetic field is a paradigmatic frustrated quantum magnet, yet its ground-state phase diagram at generic anisotropy and its thermal phase structure remain incompletely understood.
