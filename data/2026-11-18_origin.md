Title: Exact solution of the two-dimensional one-component plasma on the truncated Beltrami pseudosphere
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21652
Authors: Josep Batle
Abstract: We solve exactly the two-dimensional one-component plasma at coupling $\Gamma=2$ on the truncated Beltrami pseudosphere, a surface of constant negative curvature bounded by two parallels. A conformal map onto a flat cylinder leaves the particles interacting through the flat-cylinder potential, with the curvature entering only through the area element. The partition function becomes a product of incomplete gamma functions, the density is explicit, and all correlations are determinantal. Since the area is bounded, the large-N limit is taken at fixed shape. The free energy, obtained through the constant term with every coefficient in closed form, contains a bulk term (1/2)N ln N, a term linear in N, the flat hard-wall surface tension times the total boundary length, and a shape-dependent constant, with no ln N term. This is the conformal-anomaly prediction for Euler characteristic zero, tested here on a surface where the integrated Gaussian and boundary curvatures are separately nonzero and cancel. The expansion coincides term by term with the cylinder result of Shen and Forrester, whose universal constants are quadratures of the two boundary layers. At both walls the density tends to the flat contact value $n_b$ ln 2, from opposite sides, although the circumferences differ by a factor cosh u_max. We compare the asymptotics with the exact solution up to N=4096, and Monte Carlo simulations at $\Gamma= $1, 4 and 6, validated against the exact $\Gamma= $ 2 density, show how the boundary layers evolve with coupling.

Title: Statistical Properties of the $q$-Deformed Monolayer and Bilayer Graphene
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21675
Authors: Omar Pedraza, Erik D\'iaz-Bautista, L. A. L\'opez, Daniel O-Campa
Abstract: We investigate the statistical properties of the $q$-deformed Dirac oscillator for monolayer and bilayer graphene in the presence of a constant external magnetic field. Using $q$-deformed creation and annihilation operators, we obtain the corresponding energy spectra and eigenfunctions, as well as their probability current densities, recovering the conventional Landau-level structures in the undeformed limit. For bilayer graphene, the lowest Landau levels are explicitly constructed within the $q$-deformed framework. The partition functions for both systems are derived using zeta-function techniques and the Euler-Maclaurin formula, allowing us to determine their main thermodynamic quantities for small values of the deformation parameter. Our results show that the $q$-deformation modifies the energy spectra, the behavior of the probability current density components, and the thermodynamic response of the systems. A comparison between monolayer and bilayer graphene reveals distinct thermal behaviors, reflecting their different Landau-level structures and highlighting the influence of the $q$-deformation on their statistical properties.

Abstract: Based on the relevant scaling operators of renormalization-group theory, we establish a theoretical framework for constructing complete families of critical-sensitive fluctuation observables and their connected correlation matrices. The corresponding eigenstates of the correlation matrices reveal a hierarchy of multiscale critical correlation patterns and the leading normalized eigenvalues and their ratios show the finite-size scaling behavior. ### Paper [4]
Title: The Kitaev model on finite hyperbolic PBC clusters
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21697
Authors: Alberto Zorzato, Jiri Min\'a\v{r}
Abstract: We construct and solve the three-colored Kitaev model on finite hyperbolic lattices with periodic boundary conditions. We describe a self-contained procedure which uses finite quotients of orientation- preserving triangle groups to generate regular maps on compact genus-g surfaces. From these, one can extract their lattice connectivity, plaquette structure, homology cycles and Wilson sectors while implementing consistent boundary conditions. On the example of {8, 3} and {12, 3} lattices, we enumerate a selection of quotients with up to 72 vertices, which constitute the discrete steps in a finite-size scaling analysis. For the isotropic point, corresponding to the spin-liquid phase in the thermodynamic limit, we identify the ground state superselection sectors, quantify their deviation from the homogeneous {\pi} flux sector and show how the selected flux background depends on the quotient geometry. We then discuss how the resulting ground state sector degeneracies are entirely generated by physical lattice symmetries acting on both the plaquette fluxes and non-contractible Wilson loops. Finally, we study the behaviour of the finite-size two-vison gap and matter fermion correlations. We find that the gap does not decrease monotonically with increasing system size, while one still recovers the expected algebraic decay of the correlations at the isotropic point, even for the considered modest system sizes.

Title: Probing Phase Fluctuations in Cuprates by Multidimensional Josephson Spectroscopy
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21708
Authors: Albert Liu, Danica Pavicevic, Alex G\'omez Salvador, Marios H. Michael, Michael Fechner, Pedro M. Lozano, Qiang Li, Genda D. Gu, Eugene Demler, Andrea Cavalleri
Abstract: The normal state of cuprate superconductors is believed to host precursor electron pairing above the superconducting transition temperature. These pre-formed pairs, which extend over an unknown temperature range, are expected to lack long-range phase coherence. Because spectroscopic evidence for this hypothesis is only indirect, new methods to probe these physics are needed. Nonlinear terahertz spectroscopy allows one to probe high-order time correlation functions and is therefore sensitive to fluctuations. Measurements of third-harmonic generation along the superconducting planes have revealed responses persisting above Tc, but a clear relation between these optical responses and specific properties of the fluctuating state has not yet been established. Here, we apply angle-resolved multidimensional terahertz spectroscopy to probe the optical nonlinearity of the Josephson tunneling resonance in La1.83Sr0.17CuO4 across the superconducting to normal state transition. We find that among all third-order nonlinear responses, the non-rephasing multidimensional signal is especially sensitive to non-mean field fluctuations of the order parameter. We measure a crossover occurring at T ~ Tc between a mean-field optical nonlinearity that dominates at low temperatures and one that is mediated by short-range superconducting fluctuations. We present a theoretical framework that connects the out-of-plane optical response in both regimes to the fluctuating in-plane superconducting state, and contrast the sensitivity of different types of terahertz nonlinearities to fluctuating order.

Authors: Salvatore D. Pace, Sakura Schafer-Nameki, Carolyn Zhang
Abstract: The symmetry topological field theory (SymTFT) provides a unified framework for ordinary and generalized symmetries of quantum many-body systems. By encoding symmetry in a topological theory in one higher dimension, it separates symmetry data from the details of a specific quantum system. In this review, we give a pedagogical introduction to this framework and survey its applications to phases of quantum matter. Title: Constraints on the volume enclosed by Fermi surfaces
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21720
Authors: Subir Sachdev
Abstract: A fundamental property of a Fermi liquid (FL) is that the volume enclosed by the Fermi surface is determined by $\rho$, the total density of electrons. In a fractionalized Fermi liquid (FL*), the volume enclosed by the Fermi surface is determined by the same expression after $\rho$ has been shifted by a quantized amount, most commonly to $\rho -1$ (we assume spin rotation invariance, for simplicity). Title: Tunable Topological Charge Order in Magnetic Moir\'e Systems
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21745
Authors: Conor Smith, Yubo Yang, Shiwei Zhang, Miguel A. Morales
Abstract: Two-dimensional electrons in a strong magnetic field exhibit two seemingly incompatible ground states: incompressible quantum Hall liquids with a quantized Hall conductance, and electron crystals in which interactions drive the charge into ordered patterns. Moir\'e superlattices in transition-metal-dichalcogenide bilayers offer a tunable arena for this competition. Using neural quantum states (NQS) together with several methodological advances, we show how this competition unfolds. In particular, we demonstrate that the two end points are separated by an extended regime in which molecular charge order coexists with a quantized Hall response. We map the ground-state phase diagram of electrons at Landau-level filling $\nu_B=1$ in the presence of a moir\'e superlattice potential, with three electrons per moir\'e cell. In the deep triangular potential limit, the electrons form the topologically trivial "Wigner molecular crystal'' of three-electron artificial atoms, a state recently imaged at zero magnetic field in moir\'e bilayers. As the potential depth is lowered, molecular charge order remains while quantized Hall response emerges. At the honeycomb point, where each cell contains three electrons and two degenerate moir\'e minima, wells are either singly or doubly occupied and the doublons provide an emergent Ising degree of freedom. Starting from modest interaction strength, we find short-range doublon correlations within the Chern insulator which grow with interaction strength and eventually break sublattice symmetry, forming a N\'eel CDW with long-range order between doublons. The topological molecular regime lies at twist angles and magnetic fields directly accessible to current experiments. The methodological advances presented provide a scalable framework for exploring a broad range of questions in correlated quantum matter in the presence of magnetic fields.

Title: Hidden convexity of the Eliashberg condensation energy: existence, uniqueness and continuity of the superconducting state
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21773
Authors: Davis Rens
Abstract: Kiessling, Altshuler and Yuzbashyan conjectured the thermodynamic transition picture for the isotropic Eliashberg spin-chain functional with vanishing Coulomb pseudopotential: the normal state is the unique minimizer above and at a critical temperature, the superconducting minimizer below it is unique up to gauge, and the transition is continuous. We establish the nonlinear part of this picture. In the variables $\rho_n=\sin^2\theta_n$, the condensation energy restricted to the first quadrant is strictly convex, and folding any admissible chain into that quadrant cannot increase its energy. For every positive, non-increasing interaction with power-law decay, hidden convexity and a compactness estimate give existence and uniqueness of the minimizer up to gauge. The normal state minimizes the energy exactly when its quadratic form is non-negative, including marginal stability. Among admissible chains, a positive solution of the nonlinear Eliashberg equations exists exactly when the normal state is linearly unstable and is then unique. For temperature-dependent families with pointwise continuity and locally uniform decay bounds, the minimizing density is continuous in a weighted $\ell^1$ norm. Together with uniqueness of the linear-stability temperature, these results prove the thermodynamic conjectures for the $\gamma$-model, Einstein phonons and general phonon spectra; the phonon applications use separately established spectral input. Finite-dimensional algebraic and convex-analytic components are machine-checked in Lean. The proofs were obtained independently of concurrent work that reaches overlapping conclusions.

Title: Magnetic tuning of odd elastic instabilities in chiral active crystals

Abstract: We investigate the dynamical properties of an odd active crystal whose elementary units follow an inertial dynamics and experience a Lorentz force induced by an external magnetic field. We show that the interplay between the magnetic field and these transverse forces produces a distinctive long-wavelength instability at vanishing wavevector, which is absent in both non-magnetic elastic crystals and passive crystals subjected to a magnetic field.
