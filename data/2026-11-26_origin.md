Title: Theory of Anomalous Diffusion and Superdiffusion in Integrable Spin Chains
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33274
Authors: Jitendra Kethepalli, Andrew Urilyon, Jacopo De Nardis
Abstract: Integrable systems can exhibit diffusive or Kardar--Parisi--Zhang (KPZ) scaling in two-point correlations even when their current full counting statistics (FCS) differ from those usually associated with these universality classes. We explain this separation using a multispecies particle gas that preserves the scattering structure of integrable spin chains. For anisotropy $\Delta>1$, large particles are advected by a mobile bath. The resulting FCS has a Gaussian-mixture form and approaches the M-Wright distribution at late times, with finite-time rounding due to mobile species. At the isotropic point $\Delta=1$, all particles are mobile, and the charge transfer by large particles is discrete. A self-consistent Poisson distribution describes the FCS, with a nearly Gaussian bulk and model-dependent, non-Gaussian tails. By contrast, two-point correlations follow from the statistics of a large tracer particle. For $\Delta>1$, it undergoes Brownian motion, yielding Gaussian spreading. At $\Delta=1$, we provide evidence from fluctuating hydrodynamics for the emergence of the KPZ Pr\"ahofer--Spohn scaling function in dynamical correlations and trace it to the finite coupling of the largest particles to a continuum of slower ones, with long-ranged velocity fluctuations. Our framework shows how universal dynamical two-point correlations coexist with distinct model-dependent current statistics.

### Paper [2]
Title: Pairing from anti-Hund's coupling under infinite Hubbard repulsion
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33286
Authors: Donggyu Kim, Ya-Hui Zhang
Abstract: Electron-phonon coupling is a natural source of superconductivity, but in strongly correlated materials the phonon-mediated interaction is often much weaker than the on-site Hubbard repulsion $U$. Whether, and how, it can still produce pairing when $U$ dominates is a basic open question. We address this question by studying a minimal model with an anti-Hund's coupling $J_A$: an on-site spin exchange between two orbitals or two valleys, mediated by optical phonons, that favors a local inter-orbital spin singlet. In the limit $U=+\infty$, the two-orbital Hubbard model on the triangular lattice, doped away from the insulator at $n=2$ electrons per site, reduces to a four-flavor $t$-$J_A$ model. Density-matrix renormalization group calculations find robust pairing throughout $1\le J_A/|t|\le10$, with the largest pairing gap at $J_A\sim|t|$. The Cooper pairs reside on bonds and do not come from a direct attraction provided by $J_A$, since infinite $U$ excludes on-site pairs from the Hilbert space. For $J_A\gg|t|$, the normal state is a second Fermi liquid whose small Fermi surface violates the perturbative Luttinger theorem, and a strong-coupling expansion in $t/J_A$ provides a controlled effective theory of superconductivity from this unconventional metal. Our results provide strong evidence for a strongly coupled superconductor beyond BCS theory, driven by the interplay of the kinetic energy, the phonon-mediated anti-Hund's coupling, and the strong Hubbard $U$. Finally, we discuss possible applications to magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene.

Title: Spontaneous Time-Reversal-Symmetry-Breaking State in Proximity-Induced Superconductivity
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33288
Authors: Jonathan Schirmer, Wei Pan, Javad Shabani, Enrico Rossi
Abstract: Recent experiments on superconducting heterostructures formed by an $s$-wave superconductor and a Dirac semimetal suggest that in these systems the superconducting state can break time-reversal symmetry even though no magnetic order or unconventional pairing is present in any of the structure's components. We present a minimal mechanism for a time-reversal-symmetry-breaking superconducting state in multiband systems proximity-coupled to a conventional $s$-wave superconductor. We derive the conditions for this state, find that it is the ground state wherever it exists, and obtain the phase diagrams. We find that in some situations the phase diagrams describe reentrant behavior: by continuously varying a parameter the system can undergo multiple transitions between a time-reversal-symmetric state and a state that spontaneously breaks time-reversal symmetry. The results help identify heterostructures that can be engineered to obtain nonreciprocal superconducting transport effects even when no magnetic field or magnetization is present.

Title: Can the chemical potential and the many-body correlated phases of a two-dimensional dipolar fluid be approximated analytically? A Quantum Monte-Carlo study of a universal behavior of dipolar excitons
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33289
Authors: Yotam Mazuz-Harpaz, Michal Zimmerman, Snir Gazit, Ronen Rapaport
Abstract: A central challenge in quantum many-body physics is to connect microscopic interactions to experimentally accessible macroscopic states of matter, where correlations extend across multiple length scales. Using quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) simulations of two-dimensional repulsive dipolar bosons, recently realized across various experimental platforms, we show that correlations within the dipolar fluid lead to an unexpected simplification. Specifically, we find that these correlations induce a pronounced depletion region around each particle that suppresses short-range physics and yields an emergent universality, where the fluid is governed by dipolar interactions rather than material-specific details. Exploiting this universality, we establish a unified, quantitatively validated analytical framework for the dipolar fluid, taking indirect (interlayer) excitons as a model system. We show that analytical interaction energies derived in an earlier study agree with exact QMC results across three correlation regimes: a classical gas, a quantum gas, and a high-density liquid. In contrast, the widely used uncorrelated plate capacitor formula severely overestimates the numerical values. Building on these expressions, we show that the chemical potential is accurately captured by a three-component decomposition: an ideal Bose-gas kinetic term, a regime-dependent dipolar interaction term, and a high-density quantum confinement term. Finally, we address a key experimental challenge: extracting exciton densities from measured energy shifts. Our QMC-benchmarked calibration curves for typical gallium arsenide (GaAs) and transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) heterostructures offer a reliable tool for experimentalists to determine both the fluid's density and collective state. This universal framework extends to other two-dimensional systems, including ultracold dipolar atoms and polar molecules.

Title: Spin superfluid transport of magnons in altermagnetic insulators
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33294
Authors: Dele Li, Meng-Han Zhang, Dao-Xin Yao
Abstract: We study the spin superfluidity for Lieb and inverse Lieb lattices in altermagnets, which enables coherent transport of spin angular momentum carried by the phase of an easy-plane N\'eel order. The easy-plane anisotropy supplies the $U(1)$ platform, while the nonrelativistic spin splitting generates sublattice-alternating exchange imbalance with $B_{1g}$ symmetry. Via a continuum theory and Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert simulations, we find that the Goldstone mode becomes quadratic at the critical stability boundary. An axial phase gradient transfers phase-channel spectral weight to the gapped mode, whereas a diagonal flow leaves these signatures absent. The spin superfluid exhibits momentum-space anisotropy due to the chiral-splitting magnon dispersion, while strong fields or elevated temperatures suppress phase coherence. Accounting for the contribution of thermal magnons at the interface, we present that the critical spin flow varies with the injection angle under the altermagnetic crystal-field effects. The spin-superfluid decay length scales with the interfacial spin-mixing conductance, which can be measured by the spin Hall magnetoresistance and spin Seebeck effect. Our findings establish altermagnetic insulators, particularly $\mathrm{La_2O_3FeMnSe_2}$, as promising platforms for low-dissipation spin transport.

Title: Bosonic Magic Resources at Quantum Criticality
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33306
Authors: Paolo Stornati, Piotr Sierant, Emanuele Tirrito
Abstract: Non-Gaussianity is a resource for bosonic quantum information processing, providing a necessary ingredient for universal quantum computation in continuous-variable systems. Here we quantify non-Gaussianity in the ground state of the one-dimensional extended Bose-Hubbard model using quantum relative entropy (QRE), a measure determined solely by one-body correlations. The scaling of the QRE with system size reveals quantum phase transitions involving hidden and conventional order. We connect QRE scaling in the weakly interacting superfluid phase to Luttinger-liquid correlations and identify distinct critical signatures in its extensive and subleading contributions. At the Mott-Haldane transition, subtracting the extensive background reveals a pronounced peak in the subleading contribution, providing a clear signature of the onset of hidden string order. At the Haldane-density-wave transition, the extensive contribution to the QRE instead exhibits scaling consistent with Ising criticality. These results establish non-Gaussianity as a probe of many-body criticality, linking bosonic quantum resources to strongly correlated matter.

Title: On the binding and stability of CeO2 molecules on low-index Au surfaces
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33338
Authors: Ying-Cheng Chen, Ahmad Ahmad, Anter El-Azab
Abstract: Many multi-phase thin films grown by self-assembly involve co-deposition of both metallic and oxide phases that grow into the same film. Under such conditions, the oxide species may be present in stoichiometric molecular form on the surface of the metallic phase. Quantifying the stability, binding, and diffusion of such molecular oxide states on metallic surfaces is critical for understanding self-assembly. In this work, we investigate the binding and stability of CeO2 molecules on Au surfaces using spin-polarized density functional theory calculations. Specifically, we examine how the configuration of the CeO2 molecule, its binding energy and binding free energy, structural stability, effective charge, and projected electronic density of states vary among various binding sites on Au(001), Au(110), and Au(111) surfaces. Molecular CeO2 was found to be more favorable on all Au surfaces than its atomic constituents. It was also found that the bent CeO2 molecule configuration is more stable than the linear counterpart, with the Ce atom on the hollow site and the O atoms on the top sites. Furthermore, the charge transfer was found to occur in such a way that the CeO2 molecule becomes polarized on all surfaces, but the polarization is stronger only for the strongly bonded states on Au(001) and Au(110) surfaces. Finally, three different cases of how the CeO2 molecule binds to Au(001), Au(110), and Au(111) were identified: case 1: chemisorption with symmetric bonds, case 2: chemisorption with asymmetric bonds, and case 3: physisorption with symmetric bonds. These three cases of CeO2 binding are present on Au(001) and Au(110), but only cases 2 and 3 occur on Au(111).

Title: Extrinsic thermodynamic geometry, symmetry, and the cost of constraint
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33443
Authors: Jordan R. Sawchuk, David A. Sivak
Abstract: Driving a stochastic system in finite time costs excess work; exactly how much depends on which system parameters, and hence which paths in distribution space, a controller can access. In the friction-tensor formalism for slow driving, the space of equilibrium distributions is a Riemannian manifold and constraints on a controller define submanifolds. Whether a constrained controller is forced onto suboptimal paths is a question of the extrinsic thermodynamic geometry of the submanifold, which we develop for discrete systems. We show that submanifolds with zero extrinsic curvature can be identified from symmetries of the transition network, before any optimization. A minimum-work protocol on such a submanifold is therefore stationary in the excess work even when the constraints are lifted. The stationarity of symmetric optima holds exactly, beyond slow driving. In the slow-driving regime, we show that the ambient curvature transverse to a symmetric submanifold determines whether nearby symmetry-broken protocols have lower excess work. This curvature, which separates into independent contributions from each pattern of symmetry breaking, thus acquires an operational meaning as a measure of the thermodynamic incentive to break a given symmetry. A thermodynamic comparison theorem provides an inexpensive test of local minimality that weighs the extremal curvatures along the symmetric protocol against its excess work. There is no need for such tests under certain convexity conditions on the transition rates and control parameters, which guarantee that a symmetric optimum is a global optimum. These results certify the global optimality of a five-parameter protocol previously observed to match 15-parameter full control of a four-spin Ising system, and explain why partial symmetry breaking is optimal for the same system under local-field control.
