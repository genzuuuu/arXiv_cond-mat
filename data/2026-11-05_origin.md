Title: Shapiro spikes in driven quantum fluids
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05477
Authors: Vijay Pal Singh, Ludwig Mathey, Luigi Amico
Abstract: Periodic driving of a Josephson junction produces resonant transport signatures known as Shapiro spikes, conventionally described by effective circuit models. Here, we demonstrate Shapiro spikes in a neutral quantum fluid using simulations of a potential-biased atomic Josephson junction. The dc-current resonances follow the expected Bessel-function scaling, including the suppression and revival of individual resonances with increasing modulation strength. We uncover their microscopic mechanism by showing that the phase-winding frequency develops finite plateaus at integer multiples of the drive frequency. These phase-locked states are sustained by quantized-vortex nucleation and the associated $2\pi$ phase slips. Remarkably, within a locking plateau, the dc current and density transfer can reverse direction while the phase-winding frequency remains locked to the drive, giving rise to negative-mobility transport. Our results establish a microscopic link between vortex-mediated phase slips, Shapiro resonances, and anomalous transport in a neutral quantum fluid, revealing how quantized vortices convert resonant transport into phase-locked dynamics with negative mobility.

Title: Quasicrystals are Functionally Graded Materials
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05478
Authors: Michael J. Landry, Dominic Bollettieri, Akhil Sadam, Denisse C\'ordova Carrizales, Mingda Li
Abstract: Quasicrystals exhibit long-range order without translational periodicity, posing challenges to compute their electronic properties. Here we show that a quasicrystal can be represented exactly as a periodic approximant with a spatially graded phason, establishing a connection to functionally graded materials. Building on this connection, we develop a controlled quantum theory of quasicrystals. We derive a state-dependent control parameter governed by the quantum metric of local Bloch states, determining when finite approximations are reliable. We further construct a Hamiltonian that correctly predicts Bragg gap positions, quasimomentum equivalence, and quasiperiodic eigenstates to all orders in phason strain within a finite Fourier window. We verify the theory numerically and propose a diffraction-based experimental test of the gap shift induced by phason strain. Our framework opens a route to predicting the electronic structure and functional properties of quasicrystals using methods developed for functionally graded materials.

Title: Resonantly interacting $2e/3$ anyons: a controlled microscopic theory of anyon superconductivity
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05529
Authors: Qingchen Li, Pavel A. Nosov, Zhaoyu Han, Si-Yuan Chen, Taige Wang, Eslam Khalaf
Abstract: We develop a microscopic theory of anyon superconductivity that connects electronic interactions in a lightly doped Laughlin state to the universal physics of a resonantly interacting anyon gas. Using geometric quantization of Laughlin quasiholes, we derive the effective many-body Hamiltonian of dilute charge-$2e/3$ anyons, including their valley structure, dispersion, and statistical and short-range interactions, together with an explicit map between anyon and electron observables. This reduces the dilute many-body problem to a few parameters fixed by data computable from few anyon numerics. In analogy with resonant Fermi and Bose gases, we organize the dilute anyon problem around a two-body binding resonance and its relevant and marginal perturbations. For a three-valley gas of charge-$2e/3$ anyons, we identify a resonance whose long-distance theory is governed by an Aharonov-Casher anyon Hamiltonian whose magnetic field is the statistical Chern-Simons field and whose ground state is exactly solvable. Using the ground state wavefunction, we demonstrate Cooper-pair off-diagonal long-range order directly in electron coordinates and determine the pairing symmetry and vortex-lattice structure. The leading perturbations at the fixed point are a relevant pair detuning and a marginal three-body contact in the valley-singlet channel, which determine the compressibility and sound velocity. A repulsive three-body coupling is marginally irrelevant, allowing the fixed point to control a parametrically broad dilute regime near pair resonance, while attractive three-body interactions instead generate charge-$2e$ trimers. More broadly, our results show that binding thresholds between competing anyon composites host universal resonant regimes that are not captured by the lowest-energy anyon species alone, providing a new setting for controlled theories of anyon matter.

Title: Low cross-plane thermal conductivity of $\kappa$-Ga$_2$O$_3$ thin films
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05647
Authors: Seungwon Park, Jingyu Tang, Minsung An, Alan J. H. McGaughey, Lisa M. Porter, Robert F. Davis, Jonathan A. Malen
Abstract: The thermal conductivities of (001) $\kappa$-Ga$_2$O$_3$ thin films deposited on $c$-plane sapphire substrates were measured with frequency-domain thermoreflectance (FDTR). The thermal conductivity increases from 1.83 $^{+0.19}_{-0.18}$ W/m$\cdot$K to 2.26 $^{+0.35}_{-0.40}$ W/m$\cdot$K as the thicknesses increase from 159 nm to 689 nm. This weak thickness dependence indicates that the majority of the intrinsic phonon mean free paths are smaller than the range of investigated film thicknesses. First-principles-based calculations that include phonon-phonon and phonon-boundary scattering capture this size-dependent behavior and support our experimental observation. Temperature-dependent measurements of a 689 nm film from 293 to 400 K show a decrease in thermal conductivity with increasing temperature due to enhanced phonon-phonon scattering. A weaker temperature dependence is observed in a 159 nm film where phonon-boundary scattering becomes more prominent. At comparable film thicknesses, our measured $\kappa$-Ga$_2$O$_3$ films exhibit lower values of cross-plane thermal conductivity than the other experimentally measured Ga$_2$O$_3$ polymorphs. Our experimental observations are supported by prior predictions, which suggest that the low thermal conductivity of $\kappa$-Ga$_2$O$_3$ results from its complex crystal structure, which leads to increased anharmonicity and lower phonon group velocities relative to other polymorphs. Thermal management strategies, such as integration with high-thermal-conductivity substrates, may thus be required to mitigate self-heating for $\kappa$-Ga$_2$O$_3$-based electronic devices.

Title: Medial Model of Elastic Curve Contacts, from Clasps to Knits
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05688
Authors: Kathryn Chain, Haoye Sun, Michael S. Dimitriyev
Abstract: Materials constructed from interlocking and tangled fibers are challenging to model and understand due in part to the complex interplay between fiber shape and contact mechanics. Much of our intuition about contact mechanics comes from the Hertzian description, which is based on interactions between convex bodies. However, fibers often form clasp shapes, which involve saddle-shaped contact surfaces; the ability for two interacting bodies to conform to each other along a saddle surface confounds many existing mechanical contact models. Following an observation that contact surfaces between fibers tend to look like minimal, constant mean-curvature surfaces, we seek to test this hypothesis and provide a direct link to geometric modeling methods. To do this, we leverage the medial axis transformation, which generalizes the notion of a Voronoi tessellation to generating curves and surfaces. Through computational studies on an orthogonal clasp and a simple knit structure, we find that the medial axis transformation provides a reasonable model for contact surfaces between identical curves. We also show that a generalization of the medial axis transformation based on radical Voronoi tessellations can be used to well-approximate contact surfaces between non-identical curves. We apply this ``radical axis transform'' to curves of different radii and demonstrate a trend for surfaces to adopt finite mean curvature. Our results may point the way towards more sophisticated geometric methods for modeling tangles.

Title: Exact Mirror-Parity Selection Rule for Electron-Phonon Coupling in Two-Dimensional Crystals
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05710
Authors: Nicol\'as F. Barrera, Javiera Cabezas-Escares, Francisco Munoz, Carlos Cardenas, Julia Contreras-Garc\'ia
Abstract: The electron--phonon (e-ph) interaction underlies conventional superconductivity and plays a central role in transport and other many-body phenomena in solids. Here, we derive a symmetry selection rule for e-ph scattering in two-dimensional crystals possessing a horizontal mirror plane, $\sigma_h$. For electronic states of mirror parity $s_n(\mathbf{k})$ and $s_m(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})$, and a phonon mode of parity $\chi_\nu(\mathbf{q})$, we show that the e-ph matrix element $g_{mn,\nu}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})$ vanishes identically unless $s_n(\mathbf{k})\,\chi_\nu(\mathbf{q})\, s_m(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})=+1$. The rule follows from the covariance of the phonon-induced deformation potential under $\sigma_h$, constitutes the $C_s$ case of the general group-theoretical selection rule for matrix elements, and partitions the e-ph interaction throughout the Brillouin zone into symmetry-allowed and symmetry-forbidden scattering channels. Consequently, mirror-even phonons couple electronic states of the same mirror parity, either even--even or odd--odd, whereas mirror-odd phonons couple only states of opposite mirror parity, for both intra- and interband processes. We illustrate the selection rule through first-principles calculations for the two-dimensional superconductor $\mathrm{Sc_2BCH_4}$, where mirror parity is well defined throughout the entire Brillouin zone and symmetry-forbidden matrix elements vanish up to numerical precision. We then extend the analysis to bulk $\mathrm{MgB_2}$, where the rule applies exactly only on mirror-invariant planes of the three-dimensional Brillouin zone. Moving away from these planes removes the exact mirror constraint and activates scattering channels that are symmetry forbidden when the mirror eigenvalues are well defined. Our results establish horizontal mirror symmetry as an exact and practical filter of e-ph scattering processes.

Title: Topological localization in unentangled random braids
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05721
Authors: Daniil Fedotov, Pieter Hendrik Willem Van Der Hoek, Sergei Nechaev
Abstract: In 2015, P. Serna, G. Bunin, and A. Nahum numerically investigated fluctuations in a bunch of unentangled random threads. They showed that fluctuations of an individual thread in a braid are qualitatively different from those of a thread embedded in a medium of fixed parallel lines. In particular, Serna et al. observed a logarithmic increase in the average transverse span, $\left<|x_N|\right>$, of a thread with polymer length, $N$. This behavior highlights the collective nature of topological interactions, in which each polymer fluctuates and constrains the motion of the others. Studying this problem both numerically and analytically, we propose the following interpretation: the logarithmic regime is transient, and sufficiently long polymers exhibit topological localization, where $\left<|x_N|\right>$ approaches a finite limit at $N\to\infty$. Unlike localization induced by quenched external disorder, this phenomenon arises solely from collective topological constraints. We study braids within a locally free approximation and support the localization by analytical investigation of Brownian bridges on the asymptotic Cayley graph of a locally free group. We further conjecture that topologically induced localization emerges as a phase transition: for a small number of threads, $n \sim 2\div 5$, the span, $\left<|x_N|\right>$, grows algebraically, with the usual scaling $\left<|x_N|\right>\sim N^{1/4}$ at $N\gg 1$; by contrast, for sufficiently large $n$, each thread in a long braid ($N\gg 1$) asymptotically localizes, approaching $\left<|x_N|\right>\sim \mathrm{const}$.

Title: Shock-driven Jetting at HMX Crystal Surface Asperities
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05752
Authors: Ethan Holbrook, Matthew P. Kroonblawd, H. Keo Springer, Alejandro Strachan
Abstract: Shock-induced jetting and recompression are plausible mechanisms for hot spot formation in energetic materials, but remain poorly understood. To this end, we apply all-atom nonreactive molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of a shock-loaded sinusoidal surface asperity in the crystalline explosive HMX (octahydro-1,3,5,7-tetranitro-1,3,5,7-tetrazocine) to quantify the roles of shock strength, asperity geometry, system size, and crystallographic orientation in determining the jetting response. This work proposes a simple hypothesis for the connection between jet morphology and ambient-pressure phase diagrams that can be readily tested in other explosives and metals. It also provides a foundation for ongoing nonreactive and reactive MD simulations of jet impact and recompression to quantify the role played by jets in forming hot spots in HMX-based explosives.

Abstract: We investigate nonequilibrium coherence spreading in quenched homogeneous bosonic gases both analytically and numerically.
