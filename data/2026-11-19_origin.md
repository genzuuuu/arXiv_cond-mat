Title: Cross-probing van der Waals multiferroics
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23337
Authors: Bo Peng, Zeya Li, Han Wang, Shengdong You, Yangliu Wu, Wei Liu, Jianliang Xie, Haipeng Lu, Peiheng Zhou, Kian Ping Loh, Je-Geun Park, Hongtao Yuan, Longjiang Deng
Abstract: Multiferroic materials, with coupled electric and magnetic orders, hold the potential to enable low-power memory and logic devices by replacing electrical currents with electric fields for magnetic switching. Reducing the dimensionality of multiferroics, from 3D to 2D, has opened new frontiers in this already burgeoning field by enabling unprecedented control over magnetoelectric coupling that is beyond the reach of bulk counterparts, owing to reduced electrostatic screening and enhanced quantum fluctuations at the atomically thin limit. A notable example is a few-layer NiI2 multiferroic, which exhibits coexisting chiral spin textures and (anti)ferroelectricity, accompanied by strong magnetoelectric coupling; by simultaneously breaking time-reversal and spatial-inversion symmetry, NiI2 shows new physical properties such as unique mutual control of chiral magnetism and polarization with giant magnetoelectric coupling, ultrafast electromagnon excitations and odd-parity p-wave magnetism. However, there are experimental challenges in unambiguously identifying intrinsic multiferroicity. This Review critically examines the current landscape of 2D multiferroic materials, with an emphasis on experimental methodologies rather than material taxonomy. We assess different conventional probes for magnetism and ferroelectricity and identify key limitations in single-technique approaches. We emphasize the importance of magneto-opto-electric cross-correlative measurements in establishing intrinsic multiferroic behaviour and outline future research directions for both fundamental studies and device applications.

### Paper [2]
Title: Trapped fermions frustrate multiterminal Josephson networks
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23390
Authors: Antonio Manesco, Karsten Flensberg
Abstract: Coherent control of fermions has become a standard approach for engineering quantum devices. Here, we explore parity-induced frustration in multiterminal Josephson arrays. We focus on odd-parity Josephson trijunctions, where the presence of a trapped fermion induces a phase winding in each trijunction. Connecting these trijunctions constrains their shared phases, resulting in a long-distance phase coherence where the frustrated configurations dominate the low-energy physics. In the classical regime, these arrays map onto the Kagome XY antiferromagnet, whose established Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition yields algebraically decaying $6e$ phase correlations. We show that the $6e$ phase coherence is manifested as a pronounced third harmonic in the current-phase relation of these arrays. While quantum phase fluctuations suppress the $6e$ coherence, we show that through spin-orbit coupling they also lead to spin correlations and entanglement between the trapped fermions. Our results establish fermion-induced frustration as a route to composite superconducting coherence and phase-mediated spin entanglement in superconductor-semiconductor artificial quantum materials.

Title: Nonlinear optical response with relaxation in the velocity gauge
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23400
Authors: Carlos M. Pedrosa, Jo\~ao P. C. Ferreira, Jo\~ao Pinho, Yu. V. Bludov, B. Amorim, Jo\~ao M. Viana Parente Lopes
Abstract: We develop a framework for the evaluation of the nonlinear optical conductivity of electrons in crystals, driven by homogeneous optical fields, taking into account dissipation effects within the relaxation time approximation (RTA). The present formalism is based on Hamiltonians with minimal light-matter coupling within the velocity gauge, where the electric field is expressed in terms of a vector potential. The nonlinear conductivities are obtained by perturbatively solving the equation of motion for the single-particle reduced density matrix, which includes dissipative effects via the relaxation time approximation. We present a perturbative method to evaluate the reference equilibrium density matrix, towards which dissipation relaxes the system, which in the velocity gauge adiabatically follows the applied vector potential, and is also broadened by dissipative effects. Comparing with previous formulations based on the length gauge, the present approach has the benefit of avoiding the cumbersome evaluation of derivatives of Bloch states with respect to momentum. We test the presented method against length-gauge results, and calculate second- and third-order responses in a graphene-like tight-binding model with a staggered sublattice potential and a periodic modulation. We find that the relaxation time approximation gives results that deviate both quantitatively and qualitatively from the simple inclusion of an imaginary damping term in the frequencies, referred to as adiabatic switching. At equal relaxation parameter, the leading above-gap injection contribution differs by a factor of two; below the gap, the numerical results indicate a finite imaginary dc response in the small-relaxation limit of the relaxation-time calculation. These results show that adiabatic switching does not generally reproduce relaxation through the equation of motion, even in the small-relaxation limit.

Title: Analytical Interacting Tight-binding Model for Fractional Chern Insulators
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23409
Authors: Lexu Zhao, Jayden S. Chang, Awwab A. Azam, Jiabin Yu
Abstract: We construct a two-dimensional 2-band tight-binding model with two-body interactions and without magnetic lattice translation, in which both the hopping and interaction are exponentially local (with Gaussian decay) in real space. The interaction is built from pair annihilation operators whose coefficients are hopping amplitudes divided by positive powers of relative distance. Without any band projection, the ground states at $1/3$ filling are exactly fractional Chern insulator (FCI) states (and thus have nonzero gap) for any finite lattice size with any periodic/twist boundary conditions. The FCI ground states are analogous to the discretized version of the Laughlin-like states in the continuum Chern-ideal band, though our FCI states do not require the onsite spinor structure that is necessary in the continuum. We further show analytically that, after adding one unit cell while keeping electron number fixed, the anyonic one-quasihole states are zero-energy ground states of the full Hamiltonian for any finite system size and any quasihole position, analogous to Laughlin quasiholes under the Haldane $V_1$ pseudopotential. Numerical evidence is provided for the possible nonzero gap of the FCI states at $1/3$ filling in the thermodynamic limit, when interaction strength is much smaller than the single-particle band gap.

Title: Predicting solid-state reaction onset temperatures from high-throughput pairwise synthesis
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23527

Abstract: Solid-state synthesis still relies on heuristics to choose reaction temperatures. The most widely used, Tammann's rule, places reaction onset at two-thirds of the lowest precursor melting point, but has never been systematically tested. Using an automated laboratory, we heated 1,035 pairwise combinations of 46 common precursors at their Tammann temperatures and identified the products by x-ray diffraction. Abstract: Pretrained universal machine-learning force fields can reduce the cost of finite-temperature molecular dynamics relative to density functional theory. Title: Topological signatures of criticality: the Ising case
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23586
Authors: Tak-Shing Chan, Pawe{\l} D{\l}otko, Jacek Gulgowski, Biagio Lucini, Mateusz Mas{\l}owski, Davide Vadacchino
Abstract: Topological properties of microscopic configurations may provide additional insight into phase transitions beyond conventional thermodynamic observables. We investigate this idea in the two-dimensional Ising model on square and triangular lattices by associating cell complexes with the positive and negative spin domains and constructing an Euler-characteristic observable from their difference. We provide the expression relating this quantity to conventional observables of the Ising model. This derivation allows us to understand its expected behavior physically. As a byproduct, we show that at any finite positive temperature the expectation of the Euler characteristic exceeds a positive, lattice-size-independent bound, hence disproving a previously suggested conjecture. Monte Carlo simulations combined with multihistogram reweighting show that this spin-odd topological observable exhibits order-parameter-like behavior near the ferromagnetic transition. Finite-size scaling of its susceptibility yields pseudocritical temperatures that converge to the known critical temperature for both lattice geometries. On the square lattice, the leading-order estimate of the critical exponent obtained from the topological observable depends more strongly on the fitting range than the magnetization estimate. This behavior is consistent with stronger subleading finite-size corrections, as supported by fits including an additional correction term. On the triangular lattice, the corresponding estimate remains consistent with the theoretical Ising value. Our study suggests that topologically defined quantities such as the Euler characteristic can provide an alternative way to locate order-disorder phase transitions in the systems considered here. Since the definition of these observables is purely geometrical, they can provide useful insights in cases in which order parameters cannot be constructed.
