### Computational & Machine Learning Methods

A significant number of papers this week focus on new computational and machine learning (ML) frameworks for materials science. A major theme is the development of more efficient and interpretable models for predicting material properties and simulating dynamics.

- **Universal ML Potentials:** The **ALIGNN 2.0** framework introduces a dependency-free, pure-PyTorch implementation of a graph neural network, unifying property prediction, force fields, and spectroscopy. It shows strong performance on numerous benchmarks, including superconducting Tc and piezoelectric properties [20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19487). Separately, a new method called **truncated automatic sparse differentiation (ASD)** is proposed to efficiently compute Hessians for machine learning interatomic potentials, enabling order-of-magnitude speedups for large systems [117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20510).
- **Interpretable & Physics-Informed ML:** Researchers have used interpretable ML and symbolic regression to map the stability landscape of defective graphene, condensing a complex high-dimensional problem into a simple analytical law [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19191). In a different approach, a **physics-informed support vector kernel** based on Green's function analogies and spectral design is explored to guide kernel selection for regression of physical observables [171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11153).
- **Automated Workflows and Databases:** A review of computational materials databases highlights that methodological capability is no longer the bottleneck, but rather the "economics of trust"—the ability to compute properties cheaply and accurately enough for database-scale harvesting [40](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19833). New automated frameworks are emerging to address this, such as **ERAF4XRD**, a multi-agent system that reconstructs validated experimental X-ray diffraction records from scientific literature [169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18583), and **PolyJarvis**, an LLM-orchestrated agent for automating all-atom molecular dynamics simulations of polymers [178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02537).
- **Advances in Simulation Techniques:** A comprehensive review of **Suzuki-Trotter decompositions** summarizes the state-of-the-art in numerical methods for quantum time evolution, with a focus on improved error bounds and applications in quantum computing [97](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19184). For optimization problems, a **portfolio-based constrained multi-objective Bayesian optimization** strategy is presented for materials design, using adaptive policies that outperform fixed-policy baselines [23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19550).

### Superconductivity & Topological Matter

Research into unconventional and topological superconductivity remains a vibrant field, with new theoretical proposals and experimental observations.

- **Unconventional Pairing Mechanisms:** A theoretical study proposes a **valley-helical superconducting state** in time-reversal symmetric rhombohedral graphene, where repulsive interactions induce pairing of opposite chiralities in each valley [7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19283). Another work develops an interlayer pairing mechanism for bilayer nickelates, emphasizing the role of the half-filled $d_{z^2}$ orbital [153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15298).
- **Experimental Discoveries:** Signatures of **superconductivity at ambient pressure** have been reported in bulk single crystals of nickelates, emerging near a metal-insulator phase boundary [35](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19686). In a significant experimental advance, an "encapsulation epitaxy" technique enables the growth of large-area, air-stable **monolayer superconducting NbSe2 films**, which are then integrated into superconducting circuits for potential use in quantum devices [11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19321).
- **Topological States:** A new framework using **path-integral Monte Carlo** is developed to study **non-Abelian anyon condensation** in Kitaev quantum doubles, providing a unified numerical method for transitions out of topological order [6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19282). The **Kramers-Wannier duality** is shown to be an invertible transformation under certain conditions on finite chains, with implications for other models with the same bond algebra [53](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20090).
- **Quantum Hall & Flat Bands:** In bilayer graphene, a displacement field is predicted to stabilize even-denominator fractional quantum Hall states, including the Moore-Read state, by softening the effective Coulomb interaction [14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19379). STM experiments on the kagome ferromagnet Fe$_3$Sn$_2$ reveal a **tunable flat band** near the Fermi energy, which first-principles calculations show arises from interlayer hopping rather than intralayer destructive interference [44](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19857).

### Magnetism & Spintronics

Several papers explore complex magnetic phenomena and their potential for spintronic applications.

- **Altermagnetism & Spin Dynamics:** Real-space propagation of magnons is proposed as a fingerprint for distinguishing **altermagnetism** from conventional magnetic orders, with direction-dependent group velocities creating a distinct cross-like pattern [91](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20767). A separate theory work discusses inducing **mixed-parity spin splittings** in altermagnets using light or loop-current order [148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03026).
- **Antiferromagnetic Spintronics:** A **Dyakonov-Perel-type magnon spin relaxation mechanism** is observed in uniaxial antiferromagnetic insulators Cr2O3 and α-Fe2O3, providing insights for low-dissipation spintronic devices [36](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19700). In a related experimental observation, spin currents are injected into a superconductor via **vortex-mediated processes** in a NbSe2/ferrimagnet heterostructure [37](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19727).
- **Complex Magnetic Order:** A review discusses how **higher-order interactions** in complex systems can produce collective behaviors that are not reducible to pairwise contributions, emphasizing that representation and mechanism are distinct questions [56](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20142). Neutron scattering and first-principles calculations on a kagome family show that the low-field **1/9 magnetization plateau** is a robust feature linked to a gapless quantum-disordered ground state, distinct from the origin of the 1/3 plateau [24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19572).

### Quantum Systems & Quantum Information

Theoretical work continues to advance the understanding of quantum many-body systems, particularly in non-equilibrium settings.

- **Thermalization & Chaos:** An exact real-time example of **Lyapunov-controlled thermalization** is demonstrated in a Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model, showing that a verified fluctuation-dissipation relation does not guarantee equilibrium, with hidden memory of the initial state decaying at a rate set by the Lyapunov exponent [82](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20597). In a different study, a **deterministic hypergraph SYK model** is shown to retain melonic solvability and maximal chaos without disorder averaging, making it more amenable to quantum simulation [102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19255).
- **Open & Driven Systems:** A new class of **threshold resetting** is introduced for first-passage time optimization, where search agents are collectively reset upon reaching a threshold, inducing long-range interactions [127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13501). A theoretical framework for **quantum metrology in open systems** demonstrates how optimal observables can be constructed directly from the master equation, even for non-equilibrium properties like relaxation rates [173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23600).
- **Quantum Computing Hardware:** A new architecture for a **flopping-mode hole spin qubit in germanium** demonstrates high-fidelity single-qubit gates with ultra-low drive power, a key step for scalable hole-spin-based quantum computers [92](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20775). Progress is also reported on a **300mm CMOS-compatible quantum silicon-on-insulator (QSOI) platform**, showing highly uniform first-electron positions across a wafer [51](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20043).

### 2D Materials & Heterostructures

The study of two-dimensional materials continues to yield novel physical phenomena and engineering opportunities.

- **Novel 2D Structures:** A comprehensive first-principles study investigates the properties of a **tetragonal T-GaN monolayer**, a non-hexagonal phase with significant in-plane anisotropy and a moderate indirect bandgap, suggesting its use in anisotropic nanoelectronics [2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19192). In another work, a **multiferroic quantum dot** is engineered in a van der Waals heterostructure of ferroelectric SnTe nanoislands on the layered magnet CrBr$_2$, demonstrating coupling between quantum confinement, magnetism, and ferroelectricity [16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19408).
- **Transport & Device Physics:** Quantum transport simulations reveal that gate-defined **graphene superlattice junctions** can host a rich set of phenomena including Fabry-Pérot interference, the Hofstadter butterfly, and new "supersnake" states [29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19627). A study on strain engineering shows that an ultralow tensile strain of 0.1% in a MoSe2/ReS2 heterostructure can boost photoluminescence quantum yield eightfold via **exciton funneling** [68](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20387).
- **Twisted Bilayer Graphene:** The plasmonic response of twisted bilayer graphene is explored across dispersive and flat-band regimes using time-dependent DFT, identifying a distinct interband plasmon supported by transitions involving the quasi-flat bands [58](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20158).

### Structural, Mechanical, & Thermal Properties

New insights are being gained into the fundamental thermodynamics and mechanics of materials under various conditions.

- **Phase Transitions at High Pressure:** In situ electrical resistivity measurements show that the melting slope of corundum (α-Al2O3) **decreases** under compression up to 8 GPa, confirming a negative curvature for its melting curve [3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19229). Neural-network molecular dynamics simulations of B2-NiAl predict a thermodynamically consistent melting curve, finding that melting remains congruent (equiatomic liquid) at all studied pressures [13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19355).
- **Anharmonic & Nonlinear Effects:** A first-principles theory is developed for **phonon renormalization from nonlinear electron-phonon interactions**, showing that the one-electron-two-phonon process renormalizes phonons across the entire Brillouin zone, unlike the linear contribution [85](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20639). The power factor of monolayer SnSe is shown to have different defect tolerances depending on the phase, with anharmonic stabilization in the β-phase enabling high-temperature transport calculations [48](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20019).
- **Buckling & Fracture:** The origin of **imperfection sensitivity in the buckling of cylindrical shells** is traced to the sensitivity of the underlying bifurcation structure to the geometry of the imperfection, not just its magnitude [106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19436). A theoretical analysis of fiber-length distributions in composites revisits a hypoexponential form, finding it serves as a useful fitting model but not an exact generative distribution for the fragmentation process [61](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20312).

### Soft Matter & Active Systems

Research into soft and active matter reveals complex emergent behaviors driven by non-equilibrium dynamics.

- **Active Matter Self-Assembly:** A new scalar phase-field model, **Active Model H⁻**, is shown to produce filamentous networks with arrested coarsening, a morphology not seen in existing theories. Isolated filaments are unstable to bending and self-propel, assembling into a percolating network [39](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19800).
- **Collective Dynamics:** The onset of spontaneous flow in confined active nematics is shown to be governed by a competition between two instabilities: a long-wavelength mode (unidirectional flow) and a finite-wavelength mode (transverse rolls), establishing wavelength selection as an intrinsic feature [94](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20786). In another study, the mass and spatial extent of **dry active-matter vortices** around circular obstacles are found to follow two distinct scaling regimes, with a condensed state emerging above a critical density [89](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20743).
- **Polymer & Droplet Dynamics:** Simulations of semiflexible active polymers in a deformable droplet reveal that extensile and contractile activities lead to distinct internal organizations and droplet motilities, with activity coupling strongly to interface deformation [88](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20730).
