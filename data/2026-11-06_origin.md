Title: Where heat flows and where to change it: gauge-free sensitivity maps of thermal conductivity in disordered solids
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06958
Authors: D. A. Drabold, C. Ugwumadu
Abstract: Designing materials for thermal management requires knowing where heat flows, not only how much. Within harmonic (Allen$--$Feldman) theory we develop two local descriptions and test them on amorphous silicon. The first, a projection of the conductivity $\kappa$ onto atoms, depends on how the potential energy is divided among atoms (a "gauge"); we find the dependence moderate, 23% of the map's spatial standard deviation at single sites and 9.5% over regions of radius 0.4 nm, apart from a uniform offset of 0.8% of $\kappa$. The second avoids the division altogether. It is the exact response of $\kappa$ to the mass of each atom and the stiffness of each bond, it obeys an Euler sum rule, and it contains the heat-flow map as one of three exact terms. In amorphous and paracrystalline silicon, with ACE, GAP-18 and Keating force constants, the two maps are anti-correlated: where little heat flows a heavier atom lowers $\kappa$, and where much heat flows it tends to raise it. The explanation is local softness. The harmonic vibrational amplitude of each atom predicts both maps, and at room temperature the anti-correlation largely disappears at fixed amplitude, in a 32,768-atom replica as well; below about 100 K a component remains that softness does not capture. Thus soft regions, which carry little heat, act through the mixing of vibrational modes about 1$--$2 meV apart. In paracrystalline silicon the grains conduct more heat per atom, while the disordered matrix controls the response. Exact recalculations show that the response map guides the placement of dispersed substituents and locates the region that limits transport across an amorphous/crystalline interface.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06980
Authors: Walter Friza, Manfred Kaltenbacher
Abstract: Modeling the anisotropic hysteresis behaviour of permanent magnets requires vector hysteresis models capable of representing preferred (easy) magnetization directions. This work extends the energy-based vector play model to anisotropic materials by replacing the spherical pinning region of each particle with an ellipsoid, whose semi-axes represent direction-dependent pinning forces. Special attention is paid to the geometrically consistent adaptation of the update formula for the vector play model. The anhysteretic part of the model is also given an angular dependency. ### Paper [3]
Title: Stability and Relaxation of Electron-Phonon Systems at Strong Coupling
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07014
Authors: Noga Bashan, Erez Berg
Abstract: We study the kinetic properties of Migdal-Eliashberg theory (MET) for electron-phonon interactions at strong coupling. Previously, the system was found to be dynamically unstable at large couplings within a Markovian quantum Boltzmann approximation. Using the quasiclassical Kadanoff-Baym equation, we show that this instability is absent when non-Markovian effects are taken into account. We also analyze the long-time relaxation dynamics at strong-couplin, and find a clear imprint of the non-Markovian nature of the dynamics: in an intermediate temperature range between the Debye frequency and the coupling strength, the relaxation rate becomes linear in temperature with Planckian slope, while the leading relaxation mode acquires a large imaginary component, producing strongly oscillatory decay. Our results imply that any instability which preempts superconductivity must arise from effects beyond MET, such as phonon softening or polaronic effects at finite electronic bandwidth. This shows that the characteristic phonon frequency is not a fundamental bound on $T_c$, and leaves open a route to higher $T_c$ in multi-orbital electron-phonon systems.

Title: Agnostic and efficient detection of $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry-protected topological phases in projected entangled-pair states
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07030
Authors: Alexandre Chaduteau, Hao Chen, Juraj Hasik, Titus Neupert
Abstract: Symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases are the simplest example of quantum phases that cannot be detected by local order parameters. Their classification is by now well-established from multiple perspectives, a prominent one being via tensor network states. The practical question, however, is not mere classification, but detection: given the ground state of an interacting Hamiltonian and a symmetry group $G$ along with its representation, is there an efficient way to tell if the state is in a SPT phase with respect to $G$? While there exist such practical tests in one spatial dimension (1D), methods in 2D have so far been restricted to finite-size systems or require an explicit form of virtual symmetries of the tensor networks representing the ground state. Here, we develop an agnostic and efficient method to detect interacting $\mathbb{Z}_2$ 2D SPT phases, using an infinite projected entangled-pair (PEPS) representation of the ground state. The method is agnostic to the symmetry representation in the virtual space of the PEPS tensor and applicable independent of the bond dimension of the tensors. Moreover, it is efficient as it does not require finite-size scalings: it works directly in the thermodynamic limit. We numerically demonstrate the procedure by mapping out phase boundaries away from fixed-point wavefunctions. We extract the SPT label cocycle data explicitly from the PEPS tensor.

Title: Topological Transitions of Evanescent Waves
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07038
Authors: Ignacio Pavez, Tom Sheppard, Yoonseok Hwang, Hannah M. Price, Frank Schindler
Abstract: We study the band structure of evanescent waves, that is, the decaying or growing solutions that live within the gaps of tight-binding Hamiltonians. Although the underlying Hamiltonian is Hermitian, these modes carry complex wavevectors and therefore exhibit a non-Hermitian dispersion. Using the transfer matrix formalism in one dimension, we show that, in addition to the standard exceptional points that appear at the band edges, a different type of exceptional point can arise inside the gap, protected by spinless time-reversal symmetry. Tuning model parameters drives a topological transition where such in-gap exceptional points are nucleated in pairs, mediated by a crossing between purely imaginary momentum branches. We show that this spectral structure is diagnosed by a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ index assigned to every energy inside the gap, with the in-gap exceptional points arising as its domain walls. A generic bound state in the system has a tail that is purely exponential when its energy lies between a band edge and the nearest exceptional point, but becomes oscillatory - with a wavevector differing from the band-edge momentum - when its energy lies between the pair of in-gap exceptional points. We illustrate these results using continuum and tight-binding models, as well as an anisotropic photonic crystal.

Title: On the critical value of void percolation in high dimensions
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07196
Authors: Patrick Charbonneau, Marcus Michelen
Abstract: In the random Lorentz gas, a point tracer moves through spherical obstacles of (reduced) density $\Phi$ centered on a Poisson point process in $d$-dimensional space, $\mathbb{R}^d$, and becomes localized once the vacant space stops percolating, at $\Phi_{\rm p}(d)$. The dimensional scaling of this void percolation threshold has remained elusive. Prior bounds, conjectural ones included, ranged from $\Omega(1)$ to $O(d\ln d)$, leaving even the scaling undetermined. We here prove that $\Phi_{\rm p}(d)=\Theta(d)$, and in particular that $\tfrac12\,d \le \Phi_{\rm p}(d) \le 5\,d$ for all sufficiently large $d$. Because the mean-field dynamical transition is likewise linear, $\Phi_{\rm d}\simeq 2.40\,d$, our result fixes the activated window $[\Phi_{\rm d},\Phi_{\rm p}]$ as a bounded---rather than diverging---correction to mean-field dynamical arrest in sufficiently high $d$.

Authors: Andrew Laeuger, Nancy Aggarwal
Abstract: Levitated dielectric sensors are often treated as perturbative probes of a pre-existing optical field. We show that this picture fails for a novel sensor geometry: a dielectric plate whose transverse extent captures the full optical mode. Even an optically thin sensor with modest refractive index can heavily restructure the intracavity fields, effectively partitioning a cavity into two subcavities with substantial cross-coupling and producing new optical resonances and trapping sites with properties which deviate from the standard intuition. Conventional perturbative expressions for the cavity frequency shift and optomechanical coupling depart from the exact solution at thicknesses as small as $\sim\lambda/(10n)$. Furthermore, cavity locking decreases the coupling and increases the trap stiffness by orders of magnitude, and the coupled subcavity structure permits appreciable optical cooling at zero detuning, a distinct behavior unique to this nonperturbative regime. These effects, sharing a common origin, establish a distinct regime of cavity optomechanics in which the sensor is no longer merely a probe of the cavity field, but an element that helps define it.

Title: Generative marginal learning for finite-size molecular simulation with application to stress calculation
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25105
Authors: Zhao Ding, Cheng Yuan
Abstract: We propose generative marginal learning (GML), a framework for equilibrium sampling and local observable estimation in molecular simulations. By training a conditional diffusion model on large-system simulations, GML learns the marginal distribution of a retained region, enabling configuration generation without explicitly simulating the surrounding environment or imposing periodic boundary conditions on the retained region. We derive a marginal effective potential and the associated conditional mean-force identity, which provide the basis for both denoising and force-alignment training. As an application, we estimate the spatially averaged Irving--Kirkwood stress near a vacancy defect. Numerical tests on a two-dimensional Lennard--Jones crystal and three-dimensional BCC Fe under combined tension and shear demonstrate improved local stress accuracy relative to small periodic molecular dynamics simulations.

### Paper [201]
Title: Helium States in a CAS(2,2) Subspace Inspired by Quantum Photonics
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06448
Authors: Forouzan Mirmasoudi, Badie Ghavami
Abstract: We present a four-mode Fock-state photonics implementation of the variational quantum deflation (VQD) algorithm to calculate the ground-state and excited-state energies for the neutral helium atom in a CAS(2,2) Subspace. Its CAS(2,2) electronic Hilbert space is encoded into a bosonic Fock-space representation and subsequently optimized using a continuous-variable quantum circuit. The circuit architecture under study is constructed of single-mode phase shifts, beam splitters, and cross-Kerr interactions. The variational circuit is initialized in the Fock state $\ket{1100}$ and its performance is benchmarked against exact diagonalization of the active-space configuration-interaction (CASCI) Hamiltonian. We also extend the method to optimize from the first to three excited states of the helium atom with the aid of the variational quantum deflation $(VQD)$ method in a CAS(2,2). We then investigate the performance of this circuit architecture as a basis for implementing the VQD method. In the ideal noiseless simulation setting, we see that the survival probability of the ground and the excited states within the target $CAS$ subspace is unity, because the total photon number and the photon number in the two spin sectors $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are preserved. We demonstrate that the proposed approach enables the determination of the ground and excited-state energies within the CAS(2,2) electronic Hilbert space using a continuous-variable quantum circuit and the variational quantum deflation ($VQD$) algorithm, while satisfying the orthogonality conditions with negligible errors.
