Title: A Liquid Film Can Survive without Being Bulk-Like: Rupture and Mechanical Isotropy Set Different Thickness Limits in Lennard-Jones Films
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25008
Authors: Jose Luis Altamirano-Corona, Alfonso Lemus-Solorio, Francisco Villanueva-Mejia, Mariana Ramos-Estrada, Jose L. Rivera
Abstract: How thick must a liquid film be before its middle behaves like ordinary bulk liquid? Free-standing films are the standard geometry for computing surface properties by molecular simulation and the simplest model of soap and foam films, coatings and polymer layers, yet the question is seldom answered quantitatively. We answer it for Lennard-Jones films by comparing two criteria: whether the film survives without rupturing, and whether the lateral pressure at its center has relaxed to the bulk value. Covering three interaction ranges (cutoffs rc = 3.5-7.5 times sigma), three temperatures and lateral system sizes from 20 to 100 times sigma, we find that the two criteria give different thicknesses and respond to different parameters. Films thinner than 6-7 times sigma (about 2 nm for argon) rupture within 105 tau (~0.2 microseconds); this kinetic limit hardly depends on the interaction range but grows with the lateral size of the system, and close to it rupture is an activated event. The center becomes mechanically bulk-like only at a larger thickness h*, where the tangential anisotropy computed with the Irving-Kirkwood definition falls below 0.003 epsilon x sigma^3; h* grows from ~11 times sigma (3.8 nm) to ~15 times sigma (5 nm) as the cutoff increases from 3.5 to 7.5 times sigma, but it is nearly independent of temperature and of lateral size. Between the two limits films are long-lived yet perturbed throughout. Neither the surface tension, which shows no thickness dependence, nor the central density, whose relative deficit is about 16 times smaller than the relative anisotropy, reveals this perturbation: the stress detects confinement before the structure does.

Title: Exact Fractional Chern Insulators from Hubbard Interactions
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25010
Authors: Jonah Herzog-Arbeitman, Rui-Heng Liu, Jiangping Hu, Chen Fang
Abstract: Laughlin's wavefunction and its parent interaction, Haldane's pseudopotential, underpin our analytic understanding of fractional quantum Hall effect. They are an exact pair in the sense that the wavefunction is the unique, zero-energy ground state of its frustration-free parent Hamiltonian. In this work, we propose exact pairs of many-body wavefunctions for fractional Chern insulators in tight binding models with onsite Hubbard interactions as their parents. The key to our construction is introduction of a Landau-dual Chern band, a multi-orbital Chern band obtained from directly sampling the spin-$s$ Dirac Landau level on a Bravais lattice. The exact many-body wavefunctions are given, analytically, in terms of the matrix coordinate $X+iY+J$, where $(X,Y)$ are the lattice coordinates and $J$ is a nilpotent matrix in spin space. The constructed exact pairs cover the fractional Chern states of the Laughlin, the Read-Rezayi, and the Halperin series. Our results prove that FCI ground states exist beyond ideal bands.

Title: Umklapp-enhanced high-$T_c$ topological superconductivity mediated by magnons in $p$-wave magnets
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25015
Authors: Kristoffer Leraand, Asle Sudb{\o}
Abstract: We show that magnon exchange can induce nodal topological superconductivity in a microscopic model of a $p$-wave magnet. Solving the weak-coupling multiband gap equation for itinerant electrons exchange-coupled to localized spins, we find a nodal $p$-wave gap and a critical temperature that rises sharply as the Fermi surface approaches the magnetic Brillouin-zone boundary. In this regime, small-reduced-momentum magnons can scatter electrons between Fermi-surface segments on opposite sides of the zone. These Umklapp processes enhance pairing because the contributions from different sites in the magnetic unit cell cancel less in their electron-magnon vertices than in those for ordinary scattering at comparable reduced momenta. Momentum-resolved winding numbers predict zero-energy Majorana flat bands at symmetry-preserving edges where the bulk nodes project to distinct momenta.

Title: First order vortex-unbinding transition in 2D topological superconductors
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25018
Authors: Vera Mikheeva, Elio J. K\"onig
Abstract: We study the fate of the Berezinskii--Kosterlitz--Thouless (BKT) transition in a two-dimensional topological superconductor whose vortex cores each host an isolated Majorana zero mode, energetically well separated from the bulk gap and other bound states of the vortex. When the Majorana localization length $\xi_F$ exceeds the vortex core size $\xi$, nearby vortices acquire an additional short-range attractive channel from Majorana hybridization, modifying the vortex-antivortex interaction at short distances and generating a metastable minimum in the two-vortex free energy above the nominal $T_{BKT}$. We construct an effective field theory for the resulting dilute vortex gas, coupling the ordinary BKT phase field to an additional field encoding the Majorana-mediated interaction, and show, using renormalization-group and instanton methods, that this hybridization can drive the transition first order, with the metastable phase decaying at an exponentially small rate. We propose an array of Josephson-coupled $p+ip$ superconducting islands as a microscopic platform realizing the required hierarchy of scales. Finally, we discuss experimental signatures of this first-order transition, in particular a hysteresis loop whose width is set by the coexistence window.

Title: Chern Numbers from Symmetry-Enforced Zeros and Dispersion Shifts
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25028
Authors: Yoonseok Hwang, Henry Davenport, Frank Schindler
Abstract: Wave-function zeros and their local vorticities provide a complementary characterization of Chern topology to more commonly used Berry-curvature formulations. Using wave-function overlaps between a Chern band and freely chosen reference states, we show that crystalline symmetry can enforce their zeros and fix their vorticities modulo the rotation order from the angular momenta of the band and reference state. We further show that weak local perturbations produce band-dispersion shifts proportional to the absolute square of the overlap, allowing its zeros and local structure to be read off directly from the dispersion. Combined with crystalline symmetry, this can identify Chern topology without measuring wave-function phases. Our construction applies to multicomponent and multiband wave functions and allows reference states whose momentum-space representation may vanish at some momenta. This permits compact localized states to be used directly as reference states and makes use of their known real- and momentum-space properties in flat-band systems. The dispersion shift near an overlap zero further provides a lower bound on the quantum metric at the zero.

Title: Anyon Coherent States in Fractional Chern Insulators
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25034
Authors: Pedro Fittipaldi de Castro, Haylen Gerhard, Wladimir A. Benalcazar
Abstract: In fractional quantum Hall (FQH) systems, coherent anyonic excitations are the natural entities for describing anyon dynamics, their interactions, and braiding, yet no general expression for their wavefunctions is known beyond Laughlin's quasiholes. We introduce a systematic, operator-based construction of maximally-localized wavefunctions of anyons of either charge in FQH states and fractional Chern insulators, including those in non-ideal, non-vortexable bands. ### Paper [7]
Title: Topological (in)equivalence encoded in zeros of wavefunction overlaps
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25037
Authors: Carolina Paiva, Bruno Mera, Roni Ilan, Raquel Queiroz
Abstract: Topological equivalence between two gapped band structures is determined by whether their occupied states can be continuously deformed into one another without closing the energy gap or breaking the protecting symmetries. We show that this relative topology can be read directly from the overlap matrix between the two occupied subspaces, in a way that treats Chern and $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariants within a single framework. The determinant of the overlap matrix defines a global section of a line bundle over the Brillouin zone, which vanishes at the momenta where the map between the subspaces fails to be invertible. A singular connection, whose curvature is concentrated at these zeros, assigns each zero an integer charge given by its phase winding. In two-dimensional class A, the total charge equals the difference between the Chern numbers of the two systems. In class AII, time reversal pairs zeros of opposite charge, and the relative $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant is the parity of the total charge in half the Brillouin zone; the Kane-Mele Pfaffian is recovered as a special case. The zeros coincide with the gap closings of an interpolation between the two systems, and crystalline symmetries constrain their positions and charges. We illustrate the construction in the Haldane, Kane-Mele, and Qi-Wu-Zhang models, including cases where rotation symmetry alone enforces the zeros.

Title: Chiral Phonons Protected by Symmetry in a Strongly Anharmonic Helical Material
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25089
Authors: Supriyo Ghosh, Dipti Jasrasaria
Abstract: How chiral phonons survive anharmonic phonon-phonon interactions, which underpin lattice dynamics at finite temperature, remains poorly understood. Using an ab initio many-body approach, we study crystalline tellurium (Te), a strongly anharmonic helical crystal. We show that rotational symmetries in Te impose pseudoangular momentum selection rules on phonon-phonon interactions, restricting mode mixing and scattering at rotationally invariant symmetry points in the Brillouin zone. Title: Mixtures of a fluid with competing interactions and a simple solvent near a hard wall
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25097
Authors: Ivan Kravtsiv, Oksana Patsahan, Taras Patsahan
Abstract: We study a binary mixture of soft particles near a planar surface. The main component interacts through a competing short-range attraction and long-range repulsion (SALR) potential, modelled by a sum of three Yukawa terms, while the solvent and the cross interactions are described by two Yukawa terms. Using a field-theoretical mean-field approach we derive the coupled equations for the density profiles of both species, formulate the contact theorem for the two-component system, and obtain closed-form profiles in the mesoscopically homogeneous region by linearizing the theory and solving the associated eigenvalue problem. Their amplitudes are fixed by exact boundary conditions following from the continuity of the Yukawa potentials at the wall, which supply as many relations as there are unknowns. Although these conditions are implicit in the derivation of the contact theorem, they have not previously been used to fix the amplitudes of the linearized profiles, and they bring those profiles into quantitative agreement with the nonlinear solution. The equations are solved numerically for a fluid confined by hard walls. The theory predicts mixing of the two species far from the surface at high temperatures, and their microphase separation into alternating layers upon cooling or upon increasing the density. The mean-field profiles agree well with molecular dynamics data once the mean-field overestimate of the onset temperature of microsegregation is taken into account, and the analytical ones reproduce them closely except in the immediate vicinity of the wall.

### Paper [11]
Title: Where electrons conduct and where to change it: gauge-invariant sensitivities and the networks of sensitive bonds
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25104
Authors: D A Drabold, C Ugwumadu
Abstract: Where is the electrical current carried in a disordered solid, and where would a local change alter it? The two questions differ. We answer the second exactly: the derivative of the Kubo-Greenwood conductivity with respect to the Hamiltonian is gauge invariant and gives the response to any local change. The construction applies to any Hamiltonian. We try it on amorphous silicon, with tight-binding cells of up to 10000 atoms and density-functional cells of up to 512 atoms, and compare it with three ways of allocating the conductivity: the space-projected conductivity (SPC), KuboMap, and the local density of states (LDOS). KuboMap follows the LDOS closely (median Spearman correlation 0.97). The sensitivity does not. It ranks the response to an on-site shift accurately and the response to removing an atom poorly, and it transfers between Hamiltonians less well than the other maps. The most sensitive bonds form connected clusters. In three large networks they are tree-like filaments of 24 to 38 atoms in the valence tail and looped networks deeper in the gap; the excess over an LDOS-weighted random selection reaches 48 standard deviations and appears only when the broadening spans about five level spacings. The filament overlaps the tail eigenstate with the most weight on it, and one of the leading conduction channels, far more than a random cluster does, but no single state or channel explains it. The networks are largely lost in thermal snapshots at 300 K. Where the conductivity is carried and where it responds are different problems: local transport maps are useful for the first and limited for the second.

Title: A plane-wave auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo study of silicon point defects
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25121
Authors: Moritz Humer, Faruk Salihbegovic, Martin Schlipf, Zoran Sukurma, Georg Kresse
Abstract: Accurately predicting the energetics of point defects remains a formidable challenge in computational materials science. For the tetrahedral silicon self-interstitial, for example, previous periodic coupled-cluster [CCSD(T)] and diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) calculations disagree by about 1.2 eV. Here, we present formation energies of silicon self-interstitials and vacancies using auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo (AFQMC) in a plane-wave basis formulated within the projector-augmented wave framework, and compare them with the random-phase approximation (RPA) and CCSD(T). Using 16- and 64-atom cells, we demonstrate that an RPA-based correction effectively removes the two-body finite-size errors that remain after twist averaging in AFQMC for bulk silicon and for the dumbbell and hexagonal self-interstitials. With this correction, we obtain formation energies of 4.4 eV and 4.6 eV for these two self-interstitials in 16-atom cells, in good agreement with DMC and transcorrelated coupled-cluster results. For the tetrahedral interstitial and the Jahn--Teller-distorted vacancy, 16-atom cells are too small to obtain realistic formation energies; in 64-atom cells, we obtain 5.7 eV and 5.2 eV, respectively. With identical setups in 16-atom cells, CCSD(T) yields systematically lower formation energies than AFQMC, with the largest difference, 250 meV, for the tetrahedral interstitial. Our results indicate that the earlier overestimation of CCSD(T) for the tetrahedral interstitial stems from the finite-size treatment in the coupled-cluster workflow rather than from multi-configurational character of the defect.

Abstract: Structural symmetry, thermodynamic phase stability, and elastic-wave response provide related but distinct descriptions of a transforming solid. Equivariance ensures that the frozen elastic tangent inherits the structural stabilizer. Equality requires additional identifiability conditions. Little-group representations and degenerate perturbation theory then determine whether an acoustic doublet splits and the first nonzero splitting order $p$. Most importantly, the framework identifies the constitutive and measurement assumptions required to infer structural changes from mechanical spectra.
