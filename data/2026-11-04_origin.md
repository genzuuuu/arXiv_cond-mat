Title: Correlated quantum criticality and topological magnetoelectric response in Bi2Se3
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03670
Authors: Aniekan Magnus Ukpong
Abstract: A defining feature of quantum materials is that multiple orders govern low-energy physics and are not independent. We study Bi$_2$Se$_3$ by combining DFT with a renormalized correlated honeycomb model in which frustrated magnetic order and helical topological surface states are coupled via one control parameter. The surface states map to a honeycomb Hubbard model with frustrated $J_1$--$J_2$ exchange. Exact diagonalization up to 20 sites shows the N\'{e}el structure factor and valence-bond-solid order parameter cross at $J_2/J_1 \approx 0.22$, agreeing with QMC/DMRG, and a dynamical spin structure factor broadens near the crossover, as expected for a spinon continuum. Band inversion of the renormalized tight-binding model occurs on the same critical locus, producing jumps in the Chern number and $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant mirrored by the skyrmion charge $Q$. This gives a microscopic link: $Q$ selects the axion angle and thus the topological magnetoelectric coupling. We distinguish axion coupling, independent of skyrmion helicity, from the helicity-dependent magnetoelectric multipole coupling, whose toroidal component scales as $\sin\gamma$ and vanishes for N\'{e}el-type textures. We derive the $Q$-topological magnetoelectric energy and estimate $\sim 10^{-20}\,\mathrm{J}$ per skyrmion per cycle for few-tesla fields. The cycle is thermodynamically bounded: constant-$\theta$ cycles extract no net work, and efficiency is limited by axionic entropy change.

### Paper [2]
Title: When do machine-learning potentials need a configuration-dependent kernel? Learning long-range interactions with Fourier neural operators
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03678
Authors: Wei Chen, Gian-Marco Rignanese
Abstract: Long-range interactions in machine-learning interatomic potentials are commonly treated by coupling learned sources through either a prescribed electrostatic kernel or a learned but configuration-independent one. Whether such a kernel suffices depends on the underlying screened interaction. We address this question by using Fourier neural operators (FNOs) to learn how latent source fields, built from the descriptors of a local backbone (here MACE), interact across the simulation cell. For a synthetic ionic system with tunable screening, a prescribed Coulomb kernel underestimates the long-wavelength charge fluctuations by 43% under strong screening, whereas the FNO reproduces them within 3% and recovers 95-99% of the variance of the residual left by the local backbone. A linear FNO, which learns a configuration-independent kernel, performs equally well in the homogeneous case. Under inhomogeneous screening, rearranging the remote medium modulates the coupling between distant ions, a dependence that no configuration-independent kernel can represent. Among the models tested, only the nonlinear FNO responds to this modulation, and its force errors are 27-41% lower than those of configuration-independent models. In first-principles benchmarks of liquid water, lithium lanthanum zirconate, and Au2/MgO(001), MACE-FNO is comparable to a prescribed-kernel model for the bulk systems and more accurate for the interface. It further captures the reversal of the Au2 wetting preference induced by remote Al dopants, with deviations below 5 meV per cell from density functional theory. These results indicate that a configuration-dependent kernel, as learned by FNOs, becomes necessary once the screening medium varies, even though the energy remains quadratic in the charges for a fixed medium.

### Paper [3]
Title: Restricted triangular ice beyond the solvable manifolds: chiral ice and the critical region of dominant chiral vertices
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03760
Authors: Josep Batle
Abstract: We study two restrictions of Baxter's symmetric twenty-vertex model on the triangular lattice, in which the three classes of vertices allowed by the ice rule carry the weights $a$, $b$ and $c$: chiral ice, in which only the twelve chiral vertices are allowed, and the model that excludes only the two alternating vertices. We show that neither model belongs to any of the known exactly solvable families of the twenty-vertex model, and we characterise chiral ice as the ice model in which every vertex has exactly one through line, a property that imposes a parity constraint along every closed lattice line. We compute the residual entropies of both models with transfer matrices on cylinders of up to twelve vertices in circumference, extrapolated with the finite-size correction predicted by conformal invariance, which we adapt to the triangular geometry and verify at two exactly solved points. The number of upward arrows crossing the cylinder is conserved, and the cost of changing it shows that both models are critical, with the long-distance behaviour of a free massless field. Chiral ice is the extreme point of a region of the phase diagram in which ordering had been suggested; along two paths through that region we find the same critical behaviour at every point, with no feature at the proposed boundary of the region. Along Baxter's exactly solved curve we obtain the corresponding exponents in closed form.

Title: Thermal Phase Transition of Three Dimensional Orthogonal Metals
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03804
Authors: Inho Song, Minsu Park, Hanbit Oh, Eun-Gook Moon
Abstract: Orthogonal metals provide a paradigmatic example of strongly entangled metallic states whose transport and thermodynamic properties resemble those of conventional Fermi liquids, while their single-electron excitations remain fundamentally distinct. Despite considerable interest in their unusual ground-state properties, orthogonal metals in three spatial dimensions and their finite-temperature behavior remain relatively unexplored. Here, we investigate a three-dimensional orthogonal metal and characterize the thermodynamic and spectroscopic signatures associated with its finite-temperature phase transition. Starting from a simple model of fermions coupled to spin degrees of freedom, we perturbatively demonstrate that the thermal transition known to occur in the purely bosonic limit persists in the presence of coupling to a gapless Fermi surface. We then examine physical observables that change prominently between the coherent and orthogonal-metal regimes. In particular, we calculate the evolution of the physical-electron spectral function and identify characteristic signatures in angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy. We find a pronounced contrast in low-energy spectral weight between the coherent and orthogonal-metal regimes without requiring conventional symmetry-breaking order. Our results establish finite-temperature orthogonal-metal physics as a possible mechanism for pseudogap-like behavior in three-dimensional and quasi-two-dimensional layered materials with non-negligible interlayer hopping, which may be applied to the recent experiments in $\mathrm{Sr_2 VO_3 Fe As}$.

Title: Collective Modes of Fractional States Beyond Landau Levels
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03805
Authors: Peleg Emanuel, Yuval Oreg
Abstract: Collective modes provide a powerful probe into the properties and stability of topologically ordered and strongly interacting phases of matter, such as fractional quantum Hall states. Yet, the description of fractional-state excitations away from the Landau-level limit remains a formidable challenge. We develop a framework for describing collective excitations in the strong-coupling regime of coupled-wire constructions and show that they emerge as solitons and breathers of effective sine-Gordon theories. This approach provides a systematic route to studying collective modes in a broad class of fractional states. Applied to a coupled-wire construction of the fractional quantum Hall effect, the framework captures key features of the magnetoroton branch. It naturally extends to fractional Chern insulators, demonstrating that magnetoroton-like collective modes persist well beyond the Landau-level limit. We find that the magnetoroton gap increases as the Fubini-Study metric approaches the ideal (trace-condition) limit, highlighting the important role of quantum geometry in the stability of fractionalized phases and their collective excitations. The same trend favors the stability of the long-wavelength quadrupolar mode associated with the chiral graviton. Together, these results identify a distinct interplay between fractionalization and quantum geometry, and provide an analytical framework for studies of collective excitations in fractional topological matter.

Title: Lattice spin systems coupled to a compact U(1) gauge field with negative gauge coupling favoring frustration
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03837
Authors: Claudio Bonati, Ettore Vicari
Abstract: We investigate the phase diagram and nature of the phase transitions of three-dimensional compact lattice Abelian Higgs models with negative gauge coupling. These models describe multicomponent complex scalar fields coupled to a compact U(1) gauge field. Negative gauge coupling favors frustrated plaquette configurations, competing with the tendency of the matter ferromagnetic-like interaction to suppress frustration. Monte Carlo simulations and finite-size scaling analyses of models with one- and two-component scalar fields of unit charge reveal a substantially richer phase structure than at nonnegative gauge coupling. For two components, the O(3) transition line associated with gauge-invariant bilinear ordering extends into the negative-coupling region, and turns into a first-order transition line with decreasing $K$, toward the fully-frustrated $K\to -\infty$ limit. An analogous first-order transition line is also found for the one-component scalar matter with negative gauge coupling. Their energy-cumulant scaling is consistent with three-dimensional Ising and XY critical exponents, suggesting topological transitions in the $\mathbb Z_2$ and XY gauge universality classes, respectively. These results uncover additional phases generated by the competition between matter ordering and dynamical gauge frustration, whose physical characterization remains an open question.

Authors: Alberto Brollo
Abstract: The Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz (TBA) procedure developed by Yang-Yang provides the free energy functional to be minimized via a variational problem. Their result not only gives the correct thermodynamics, but it also sets the framework for Generalized Hydrodynamics. Originally developed for quantum systems, this procedure has been recently successfully applied to (integrable) classical particle systems. However, this has never been done for classical field theories: no classical derivation has consistently accounted for solitons, breathers, radiation (dispersive modes), and their mutual interactions. Title: Stationary distribution of active Brownian particles in a bounded domain
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03865
Authors: Alexandra Tzella, Daniel Loghin, David J. Needham, Jacques Vanneste
Abstract: Self-propelled particles such as swimming micro-organisms often accumulate at boundaries. Active Brownian particles (ABPs), that is, particles moving at constant speed with an orientation undergoing rotational diffusion, provide a simple model. ABPs have a finite probability to be on the boundary. Their probability density therefore includes both regular bulk and singular boundary components. Exchanges between these represent capture at the boundary and re-entry into the bulk.
  We examine the distribution of ABPs in the stationary regime. We first obtain a similarity solution that describes the probability density near the turning points, where orientation is tangential to the boundary and particles re-enter the bulk. This shows that the bulk density is singular and the boundary density is non-differentiable. We then add spatial diffusion to the ABP model and investigate the limit $\eps \to 0$ of small spatial diffusivity. We use matched asymptotics to describe the regularising effect of a finite $\eps$, focusing on the vicinity of the turning points. We assess our predictions against numerical simulations using both lattice Markov chain and finite-element discretisations.

Title: Liouvillian Kramers Degeneracy
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03881
Authors: Kohei Kawabata
Abstract: The Kramers theorem generally guarantees the spectral degeneracy of time-reversal-invariant Hamiltonian operators with half-integer spin, underlying a variety of physical phenomena. Despite its fundamental significance, the Kramers degeneracy has been argued to be absent in Liouvillian superoperators acting on mixed states because of the double structure of the Hilbert space. Here, we instead demonstrate that the interplay of time-reversal symmetry$^{\dag}$ and parity symmetry enforces the Kramers degeneracy in Liouvillians.
